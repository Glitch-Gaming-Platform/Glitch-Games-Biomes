# PART I — THE OLD PACT

The town of Harthmere is the visible top of a much older story. Almost no one alive in town knows the truth. Even the priests know only fragments. The player will, by the end of the main quest, know more than any living mortal except perhaps the dragon they are about to fight.

## I.1 The Age Before Towns

Long before Harthmere had a bridge, before the river even had its current name, the continent of **Vaerlund** was a different world. Dragons walked the surface. Not metaphor. Not myth. They walked the surface, slept in mountains, fed on entire valleys, and laid eggs in lakes that boiled around the shell.

The dragons of that age were not many — perhaps a few dozen across the whole continent — but they were enormous, intelligent, ancient, and competitive. Each treated a region of the world as a personal kingdom. Mortals lived between them, surviving by being small and forgettable.

This was the Wyrm Age. It lasted, by chapel reckoning, around two thousand years. There are no human written records from this period because the dragons did not permit any mortal civilization that grew tall enough to be noticed.

## I.2 The First Bellbinders

The change came from a strange source: a loose order of stonemasons, bell-casters, and singers who lived in the river valleys of what is now central Vaerlund. They called themselves the **Bellbinders**, though some surviving fragments call them the **Hollow Choir**, the **Tongue Wardens**, or simply *Verith-an*, "those who tune the deep."

The Bellbinders discovered three things, in this order:

1. **Dragons are sustained, in part, by their own roaring.** A dragon's voice is not merely sound — it is a kind of self-binding spell that ties the dragon to its body, its kingdom, and its hunger. A dragon that cannot roar slowly weakens.

2. **Bell-metal — specifically, alloys of copper, tin, and a trace of meteoric iron — when shaped into a great bell and struck in a precise tone, can produce a sound that physically interferes with a dragon's roar.** Not silencing it. *Untuning* it. The dragon's own voice begins to work against itself.

3. **A dragon, if continuously untuned for long enough, falls into a sleep that approaches death without quite reaching it.** And in that sleep, the dragon can be moved, sunk, buried, and built over. The dragon does not die. It dreams. And as long as the bell continues to ring, the dream continues.

The Bellbinders began with a single experiment. They lured a smaller dragon — a river wyrm called *Aevith the Slow* — into a stone amphitheater. They rang a great bronze bell at the precise tone. The wyrm fell. They buried it. They built a watchtower over its grave and made the watchtower's morning bell the same tone.

That watchtower is now buried under what is today a small farming town called **Aevithsford**. The town's name still carries the wyrm's name. The townsfolk think Aevith was a beloved horse.

## I.3 The Seven

Encouraged by this first success, the Bellbinders organized. They identified **seven dragons** whose territories overlapped with the most viable river valleys for human settlement. Over the next two generations, in a campaign of patience, secrecy, and song, they bound all seven.

The Seven Bellwards, in order of binding:

| Order | Bellward Name | Dragon Bound | Aspect | Modern Region |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|---------------|
| 1st | The First Ward | Aevith the Slow | River-wyrm | Aevithsford (farming town, lost) |
| 2nd | The Stone Ward | Karag-Drath | Mountain-wyrm | Holds of Drathmar (dwarven city) |
| 3rd | The Bell Ward | **Thaedryn the Tongue-Lost** | **River/Stone Wyrm** | **Harthmere** |
| 4th | The Ash Ward | Vyrenia of the Long Wing | Sky-wyrm | The Aetherspire (ruined high city) |
| 5th | The Salt Ward | Murvath the Tide-Coiled | Sea-wyrm | Coastal city of Pellmarra |
| 6th | The Green Ward | Sylenne the Root-Throated | Forest-wyrm | Deep Wood beneath the city of Eshen |
| 7th | The Fire Ward | Korruthax the Slag-Heart | Volcanic-wyrm | The Smoldering Isle of Khaem |

The Seven Bellwards form a rough crescent across central and southern Vaerlund. Each ward is a settlement, each settlement has a Great Bell, and each Great Bell rings in the *same fundamental tone* — though local variation in casting means each has its own overtones. (The bell at Harthmere is, was, and will again be the loudest in its register: a deep low E with a peculiar sad timbre that bell-historians call *the harthmere lean*.)

The Bellbinders disbanded after the Seventh binding. Some say they were murdered by a king who feared their power. Some say they walked into the Deep Wood and never came out. Most likely: they died of old age, one at a time, having taught only fragments of their craft to apprentices.

The bells remained. Each town grew up around its bell. The bell became a religious object, a civic landmark, an architectural feature. The reason for it was forgotten.

## I.4 Old Harth, the Last Bellbinder

The man called **Old Harth** in modern folklore was, in fact, the last living member of the Bellbinder order. He was not a bridge-keeper. The bridge story is a comfortable lie he himself spread, in his final years, to keep mortal attention away from the truth.

Harth was a bell-caster from the Aevithsford line. When he arrived at the chosen valley to bind Thaedryn, he was already old. He spent forty years working. The binding itself — luring the dragon, ringing the great bell, sinking the wyrm into the bedrock beneath the riverbed — took only a week. The forty years was everything else: casting the bell, training a generation of bell-ringers, building the original chapel atop the binding chamber, sealing the entry stairs, planting the first market, and rewriting his own story so that nobody who came after him would dig.

Harth told the next generation that he was simply the first man to charge a toll at the bridge. He told them that the chapel was just a chapel. He told them that the great bell was just a bell. He told them this so often, and so calmly, that by the time he died, even his own apprentices half-believed him.

He is buried beneath the chapel. His tomb is sealed. The seal is itself part of the binding. To open the tomb is to weaken Thaedryn's sleep. **This will matter.**

## I.5 What Went Wrong

For roughly nine hundred years, the Bell Ward at Harthmere functioned exactly as designed. The chapel rang its bell every dawn, midday, and dusk. The dragon dreamed. The town grew.

Then, three years ago, the bell rang on its own.

It was a stormy night. Father Aldren was, at that time, a junior priest. The senior priest was a woman named **Mother Halene Brae**, a sharp-witted ex-archivist who had risen to the head of the chapel late in life. Halene heard the bell ring at midnight without anyone in the tower. She climbed the tower in the rain and found the bell rope coiled neatly, untouched. The bell rang once more as she stood beside it.

Halene, alone in the dark, began to understand. She did what she thought was prudent: she had the bell removed from the tower and **buried beneath the chapel altar**, on consecrated ground, in an iron-lined pit. She told the town the bell had been stolen. She enforced silence on the few priests who knew. She prayed to Saint Verena every night for forgiveness.

She did not know, because the Bellbinders' true knowledge was lost, that **removing the bell from the tower did not stop it from binding — but moved its center.** The binding was now anchored in the buried bell, deep below the chapel, much closer to Thaedryn. The dragon's sleep grew more violent. The dragon began to *dream awake*. Its dreams began leaking into the town.

Halene died of a stroke six months later, with the secret. Father Aldren inherited her duties. He inherited her panic. He has spent the last two and a half years trying, alone, to understand what is happening, with only fragments of Halene's notes and his own faith.

He is failing.

## I.6 What The Player Will Eventually Learn

By the end of the main quest, the player will have pieced together:

- The town was built to bind a dragon.
- The bell was the binding's tongue.
- The chapel was the binding's hand.
- Old Harth was the binding's master, and his tomb is the binding's heart.
- The bell was hidden three years ago, by a frightened priest, and that act began the weakening.
- The dragon, Thaedryn, has been dreaming of the player, the chapel, the town, and the river for three years, and has, in those dreams, taught itself how to speak.
- It is also speaking to its six siblings, in their wards, across the continent.
- And they are listening.

This is the foundation. Everything in Part II builds on it.

# PART II — THE MAIN QUEST: THE BELLBOUND

## II.1 Design Principles for the Main Quest

The main quest must satisfy several MMO design pillars simultaneously:

1. **Onboarding-compatible.** A new player who happens onto the first beat should not be overwhelmed. The first hour is investigative, low-combat, and uses only services and skills the player has already learned in starter content.
2. **Escalating in scope.** Each hour should add a new layer — first the town, then the chapel, then the tomb, then the underways, then the Bellward, then the dragon.
3. **Faction-respectful.** The five faction storylines (Town Watch, Merchant Compact, Chapel Circle, River Knots, Mudden Kin) should all touch the main story in some way. The player should feel their faction choices matter, but no faction lockout should prevent completion.
4. **Group-permissive.** Hours 5 and 6 should support group play (3-5 players) but be soloable at level. Hours 1-4 are solo by default with optional shared-step credit.
5. **Reversible until the climax.** The player should be able to walk away at any beat before Hour 6 Quest 2 ("The Last Ringing") and pick the quest back up later. Past that point, the climax must play out.
6. **World-changing on completion.** Once the player completes the climax, the town visibly changes for them, the chapel state shifts permanently, and new continent-wide content opens up.

## II.3 HOUR ONE — The First Tremors

### Q1: Cracks in the Bridge
**Quest giver:** Reeve Caldus Merrow (via summons from the Reeve's Hall clerk on Noble Rise)
**Level band:** Any (designed for level 5+)
**Type:** Solo investigation, 25-35 minutes
**Pre-requisite:** Player has completed at least one starter quest in Harthmere (any faction)

#### Trigger
The first time a qualifying player crosses the Harthmere bridge after sunset, a small earthquake hits the town. Wine cups rattle in the tavern. A single market awning collapses. A child in the Mudden Ward starts crying and doesn't stop for an hour. The tremor is brief — three seconds — but everyone felt it.

The next morning, a Reeve's Hall clerk approaches the player at any major town hub (market fountain, inn, or chapel) and asks them to come speak to the Reeve about "a matter of civic concern."

#### Scene 1 — At the Reeve's Hall
Caldus Merrow meets the player on the balcony of Reeve's Hall. He is dressed in plain clothes — no chain of office today — and is visibly worried in a way the player will not have seen in any prior interaction.

> **Caldus:** "I appreciate you coming. I would normally entrust this to the Watch, but Sergeant Holt's hands are full with the gate, and I want someone with a clear head. The bridge has cracks. Not the usual kind. Not the kind that come from frost or wagons. I had Master Vale's apprentice draw them out for me." *(He hands the player a folded parchment.)* "Look at the pattern."

The parchment shows the cracks. They are not random. They radiate from a central point near the bridge's middle span. The pattern is unmistakably *bell-shaped* — the outline of an enormous bell, traced in fractures across the bridge's stones.

> **Caldus:** "Bridges crack. I know that. But bridges do not crack in *shapes*. I want you to walk it. Walk the cracks. Stand at the middle. Tell me if you feel anything strange. And — please — say nothing to the market about it. I will not have a panic over a fashionable shape in stone."

#### Scene 2 — On the Bridge
The player walks to the bridge. The cracks are visible if examined closely. A new interactable appears at the center of the bridge: a small bronze disc set into the cobbles, half-worn, with carved spirals.

When the player stands on the disc, a brief ambient audio cue plays: a low bell tone, almost inaudible, with a long, sad decay. The player's screen vibrates faintly. A status effect briefly appears in the player's UI: *"You hear something below."* It fades after eight seconds.

If the player walks away and comes back, the bronze disc no longer responds. The town's listening only happens once per player per quest.

#### Scene 3 — The Stonemason's Yard
The player returns to Caldus. He sends them to **Master Garrik Fen** at the carpenter's yard for an additional opinion. Garrik examines the parchment and laughs without humor.

> **Garrik:** "I know that pattern. So do you, if you've ever rung a chapel bell. That's not a crack pattern. That's a frequency pattern. Something rang that bridge. Something big and slow. Stones crack in those shapes when a great bell is struck very close to them."
>
> *He looks up.*
>
> "But the bell is gone. So what rang the bridge?"

Garrik instructs the player to take the parchment and the question to **Father Aldren** at the chapel. He himself will not go. When asked why, he says: "I don't want to be told I'm right."

#### Reward
- 50 silver
- "Bridgewalker's Eye" — a passive insight ability that highlights significant cracks, seams, and old carved stones for the rest of the main quest
- Caldus reputation +100 (Merchant Compact)
- Quest log update: speak to Father Aldren

#### Branching note
A player with high **Mudden Kin** reputation can, before going to the chapel, take the parchment to **Nessa Crowe**, who immediately recognizes the pattern but refuses to say from where. She says: "I'll tell you the day you trust me more than you trust the priest. Until then, go ask him." This unlocks a Nessa-specific dialogue branch later.

### Q2: Whispers at the Well
**Quest giver:** Father Aldren (Temple Green)
**Level band:** Any
**Type:** Solo investigation, 20-30 minutes

#### Scene 1 — At the Chapel
Father Aldren receives the player in the small chapel office. He is paler than usual and has not slept well. He looks at the bridge parchment for a long time without speaking. When he finally speaks, his voice is too steady, the way a man's voice gets when he is trying not to be heard shaking.

> **Aldren:** "Yes. I have seen this pattern. I have prayed about this pattern. Saint Verena has not, so far, granted me clarity. I had hoped not to involve a stranger in this. But you are here now, and the bridge is cracking, and I cannot pretend the only thing happening is the bridge."
>
> *He opens a drawer. He does not take anything out of it.*
>
> "Three days ago, two children from the Mudden Ward went to the Old Well on a dare. They said the well whispered their names. They were not believed. The next day, the same two children went again, and this time one of them — a boy named Tam — came back trembling and could not speak for an hour. When he could speak, he said the well had asked him to bring it a bell.
>
> "I tell you this because the bridge and the well are speaking, and they are speaking with the same voice."

Aldren asks the player to visit the Old Well and listen, but to bring no metal, no bell, no chime, and no charm. He instructs the player explicitly: **do not answer the well.**

#### Scene 2 — At the Old Well
The Old Well sits in a small enclosure behind the market and chapel boundary, near the Underways entry. It is sealed with iron bars. Moss covers the stones. A small audio loop of *faint dripping water* plays as the player approaches.

When the player examines the well closely, an interactable prompt appears: **"Listen at the well."**

If the player listens, the screen darkens slightly at the edges. A voice — slow, low, female-coded, and not quite human — speaks directly into the player's audio channel:

> *"Who is at my mouth?"*
>
> *"Are you the one who brings the bell?"*
>
> *"I have been waiting for a tongue to ring me."*

The voice does not respond to player chat or emotes. It speaks for about twelve seconds, then stops. The player gains a buff icon: *"Heard the Well."* This buff is permanent for the rest of the main quest and is used as a prerequisite tag later.

If the player has already done the Nessa branch from Q1 — i.e., they listened to Nessa first — they receive an alternate line at the end of the well voice:

> *"You have been with the rat-girl. She remembers. Ask her about the bell."*

This unlocks a secret optional beat (Q2.5, below).

#### Q2.5 (Optional) — The Rat-Girl Knows
**Quest giver:** Voice from the Old Well (auto-injects into log)
**Type:** Optional side path within main quest

If unlocked, the player can return to Nessa Crowe. She is initially defensive, then, when the player describes the well voice in detail, she breaks.

> **Nessa:** "Fine. *Fine.* I was nine. Eight years old, almost nine. There was a drought, and the well was lower, and my brother dared me. I dropped in. Not on purpose. I almost died. While I was down there, in the dark, I heard a woman's voice. She told me her name. She told me she had been asleep for a very long time and she was tired. She told me what was beneath the chapel. And then men pulled me up and I went home and I never told anyone, because who would believe me, and what would they do to a girl from the Ward who claimed she had spoken to a god?
>
> "I have not gone near that well in fifteen years. And I am telling you this now because if she's speaking again, then we are all about to find out I wasn't lying."

This dialogue is the player's first explicit hint that something living and intelligent is beneath the town. Nessa gives the player a small bronze charm — her brother's, who is now dead — and tells the player to keep it on them.

The charm is the **Rat-Girl's Token**, which grants a small +5% damage bonus against undead and a +10% resistance to fear effects.

#### Scene 3 — Return to Aldren
Aldren reads the player's expression and does not press them on details. He tells the player to come back tomorrow morning. He says: "I need to make a decision. I will tell you in the morning whether to keep walking down this road or to forget what you have heard."

The quest log advances with a soft 12-hour timer (gated by an in-game day cycle or a real-time skip). When the player returns next morning, Hour Two begins.

#### Reward
- 75 silver
- "Heard the Well" persistent buff
- Optional: Rat-Girl's Token (if Q2.5 done)
- Chapel reputation +100, Mudden Kin reputation +50 (if Q2.5 done)

## II.4 HOUR TWO — Tending to the Wounded Town

### Q3: Dreams of Drowning
**Quest giver:** Father Aldren
**Level band:** Any
**Type:** Social investigation, 30-40 minutes

#### Scene 1 — At the Chapel
Aldren has decided. He looks slightly more rested but also slightly more frightened. He explains:

> **Aldren:** "I have prayed. I believe — and I will say this to you and to no one else in this town — that I have been hiding a truth from myself for nearly three years. I will need to confirm it before I can act on it. To do that, I need you to do something simple and quiet, and I cannot do it myself, because everyone in town knows my face.
>
> "I want you to talk to seven people. I will give you their names. I want you to ask each of them one question, gently, as a stranger asking after their health. The question is: *'Have you been sleeping well?'* That is all. If they say no, ask what they have been dreaming. Bring me back what you hear."

He hands the player a parchment list of seven names:

1. **Elowen Pike**, the innkeeper at the Copper Kettle
2. **Mara Thistle**, the market trader
3. **Tovin Reed**, the dockmaster
4. **Old Jory**, the stable master
5. **Ysabet Fenlow**, the apothecary
6. **A child named Lina Reed**, Tovin's daughter
7. **Mother Halene's old assistant, a man named Brother Vance**, who lives now in a small cottage on the chapel grounds

The player can do these in any order. Each NPC has unique dialogue. Their dreams are designed to thematically converge.

#### Scene 2 — The Seven Conversations

**Elowen Pike (Copper Kettle Inn)**
> "Sleeping? *(laughs)* Not really, dear. I've had the strangest sort of dream. I'm standing in the river. Up to my knees. The water is colder than river water should be, and there's a sound under it — like a bell, but the wrong way around, as if the bell is at the bottom and ringing up at me through the water. I keep thinking I'm going to drown, but the water never gets higher. It just gets louder."

**Mara Thistle (Market Square)**
> "Funny you ask. Bad dreams. I'm at the market, but the stalls are empty, and the fountain is full of black water. The water is whispering, but I can't make out the words. When I wake up I always check the fountain. It's always fine. But I check anyway."

**Tovin Reed (Docks)**
> *(He is reluctant at first. The player needs to press, or use Dock-faction reputation.)*
> "All right. Yes. I've had the same dream every night for two weeks. I'm in my boat, alone, and the river is flowing the wrong direction. Toward the mountains. The boat won't turn. There's something under the water following me, and I never see it, but I know it's the size of the whole river. I always wake up before it surfaces."

**Old Jory (Stables)**
> "The horses. The horses won't sleep. It's not me. It's them. *I* sleep fine. The horses won't lie down. They have not lain down for, oh, twelve nights. They keep their heads pointed at the chapel. I have to keep stroking their necks or they shake. Something is wrong with the ground, my friend. The horses know it. Tell the priest, if you see him."

**Ysabet Fenlow (Apothecary)**
> *(She listens to the question, considers it carefully, then sits down.)*
> "I will tell you the truth, because you have a kind face and I am tired of lying. I have not slept properly in months. I dream of an enormous woman. She is sitting at the bottom of the river. She is so large that her shoulders are the riverbanks. She is sleeping, but her eyes move under her eyelids. I dream this every night. And every night I tell myself it is a dream. And every morning I am less sure."

**Lina Reed (around the docks, age eight)**
> *(The player must speak to her gently. She is timid.)*
> "There is a lady in the river. She is nice. She told me her name but I forgot. She said she is sad because she is asleep and her brother won't talk to her anymore. She said her brother lives very far away in a mountain. She told me to tell my daddy not to go on the boat at night, but he doesn't listen."

**Brother Vance (small cottage on chapel grounds)**
> *(Vance is in his sixties, half-blind from cataracts, and Mother Halene's last surviving confidant. He is the most important conversation in this quest.)*
> "She told you to come, didn't she. I always wondered who'd be the one. Sit. Drink the tea — it's not poisoned, it's just bad, I haven't been able to see the cinnamon jar in three years.
>
> "I'll tell you what I told no one. Mother Halene died of a stroke. But she had the stroke because she rang the bell. *Herself*. With her bare hands. The night before she died, she went down into the chapel cellar and she struck the buried bell with a candlestick. Just once. She wanted to know if it would answer her. It answered her. She had the stroke twelve hours later.
>
> "I have not told the priest because I am not certain he can carry it. But you go and tell him from me: the bell is still alive. And it remembers Halene. And it is angry."

#### Scene 3 — Return to Aldren
The player returns with the dreams. Aldren listens in silence. By the end, he is sitting on the chapel floor, not on a bench. He says, finally:

> **Aldren:** "She rang the bell. Halene rang it. Of course she did. She would have. She had to be sure. And then she — she —
>
> *(long pause)*
>
> "She told me the bell was stolen. She lied to my face. She did it because she did not know what else to do. And I have spent two years angry at the wrong people.
>
> "I owe you an explanation. I owe you several. But first, I owe you access to something I have kept locked. You will need to read what Halene wrote. Come with me."

#### Reward
- 100 silver
- Chapel reputation +200
- Aldren's trust unlocked (changes his dialogue across the rest of the game)
- "Dreams of the Bell" lore codex entry

### Q4: The Sister's Letters
**Quest giver:** Father Aldren
**Level band:** Any
**Type:** Lore / Reading puzzle, 25-35 minutes

#### Scene 1 — The Chapel Archive
Aldren leads the player into the chapel archive — a small wood-paneled room behind the altar, lit by a single window, lined with shelves. He unlocks a small iron-bound cabinet and removes a leather-bound bundle of letters tied with blue ribbon. Mother Halene's hand.

> **Aldren:** "She wrote to herself. It is not unusual for older clergy to do this — to dictate prayers and worries to paper as a way of clearing the mind. After her death I read the first three letters and stopped. I should not have stopped. I am asking you to read the rest with me. Three of us, in the room, in case one of us cannot bear it. Sit."

The player sits. Aldren reads the first letter aloud. Then Brother Vance arrives, having been summoned, and reads the second. The player is invited to read the third themselves.

#### Scene 2 — The Letters (as a sequential reading minigame)
Each letter unlocks a piece of lore. The player reads them in order. A few key excerpts:

**Letter 1** (Halene's voice, three years ago, dated Spring):
> *"The bell rang last night without a hand. I have written this down because I do not trust my memory. I will know in the morning if I dreamed it. I do not think I dreamed it."*

**Letter 4**:
> *"I have read the founding texts again. The chapel was built by a man named Harth — not Old Harth, just Harth — who appears in no other record but ours. He is buried beneath this floor. I know this because the floor map in the third volume of the Verenine Annals shows a tomb stone where there is now only flagstone. Someone, at some point, covered the tomb."*

**Letter 7**:
> *"I have spoken with the bell. I do not know how else to write this. I struck it with a candlestick — gently, just to see — and the room around me grew cold and a woman's voice spoke into my mind. She said her name. I will not write it here. She said she is sleeping and that the bell is the only reason her sleep continues. She said that if the bell is broken, she will wake. She said it as a kindness. She said it as a warning. I cannot tell which."*

**Letter 9** (the last letter, the day before Halene's death):
> *"I am going to ring it again. Properly this time. With both hands, with intention. I am going to ask her to stay asleep. I do not think she will answer me with kindness this time. But I would rather know.*
>
> *If anyone reads this — if Aldren reads this — I am sorry. I have lied to you. The bell is not stolen. The bell is in the iron pit beneath the altar. I had it buried. I thought I was doing the right thing. I may have done a very wrong thing.*
>
> *Tell the town nothing. Tell the bishop nothing. The town has lived above her for nine hundred years. They cannot be told.*
>
> *Pray for me, Aldren."*

#### Scene 3 — The Decision
After the ninth letter, Aldren stands. He has been crying quietly through the last three. Vance has not. Vance has been smiling — the awful, peaceful smile of a man whose suspicions have all just been confirmed.

> **Aldren:** "Then it is true. I cannot pretend it is not. The chapel was built over a tomb, and the tomb was built over a binding, and the binding is failing. I will need help. Real help, not just yours, though yours has been more than I have any right to ask.
>
> "There is a door in the chapel cellar. It has been bricked up since before my time. I have known it was there. I have prayed it would stay shut. I am going to ask you to help me open it. But first I need you to bring me something that the man buried beneath my altar would have recognized as a key.
>
> "Go to Master Vale at the Black Anvil. Tell him I have sent you. Show him this." *(He hands the player a small bronze fragment — a piece of the buried bell, broken off, perhaps by Halene's candlestick.)* "He will know what to do with it. He has been waiting longer than he has admitted to be asked."

#### Reward
- 150 silver
- Lore codex: "The Halene Letters" (all 9 readable in inventory)
- Chapel reputation +200
- "Bell-Fragment" quest item
- Quest log update: take fragment to Master Osric Vale

## II.5 HOUR THREE — The Old Pact Revealed

### Q5: Beneath the Stones
**Quest giver:** Master Osric Vale (via Father Aldren's referral)
**Level band:** Level 7+
**Type:** Crafting + Combat hybrid, 35-45 minutes

#### Scene 1 — The Black Anvil Smithy
The player brings the bell fragment to Osric Vale. Osric, who in every prior interaction has been gruff, quiet, and economical with words, looks at the fragment, then at the player, then at the fragment again, and then says, more softly than the player has heard him speak:

> **Osric:** "Close the door."

The player closes the door. Osric locks it. He sets the fragment on the anvil, takes a deep breath, and begins.

> **Osric:** "My grandfather made this bell. Not the one in the tower — that one was made before any of us. The buried one. The bell beneath the bell. He made it in 1247 by the old reckoning, and he was paid in iron and silence. I have known about it since I was eleven. I have never spoken of it, and I have never been asked.
>
> "I was wondering when someone would come. I thought it might be my apprentice, or one of the Watch. I did not expect a stranger. But here you are.
>
> "If Father Aldren sent you with this fragment, then he means to open the chapel cellar. And if he means to open the chapel cellar, then someone — you, probably — is going to need a key. The key is not a key. The key is a bell-ringer. A handheld bell, cast in the same tone as the buried one. You strike it at the door, and the door listens. We are going to forge one. Tonight. You and I. Get the bellows."

#### Scene 2 — The Forge Minigame
This is the player's first dedicated main-quest crafting sequence. It functions as a four-step minigame:

1. **Smelt the meteoric trace.** The player must source a small amount of meteoric iron. Osric reveals he has been saving a single sky-stone shard from a meteorite that fell near the Charcoal Burners' camp twelve years ago. He hands the player his tongs and walks them through the first heat.
2. **Match the alloy.** The player must add copper and tin in specific ratios. The minigame is a simple ratio-balancing puzzle. Osric coaches.
3. **Strike the form.** The player strikes the metal at the anvil. Eight strikes, on rhythm. (This uses a simple rhythm input; failure produces a slightly weaker bell with reduced damage in the boss fight, but does not block progress.)
4. **Tune the bell.** The final step: Osric rings a tuning fork. The player rings the new handbell. The two must harmonize. The player adjusts the bell's rim by gentle hammering until the tone matches.

When the bell is finished, it glows faintly in the player's inventory. It is called the **Bellbinder's Voice**, a quest-bound handbell. It cannot be sold, dropped, or traded.

#### Scene 3 — Ambush at the Forge
As Osric hands the player the bell, the smithy door rattles. Someone is testing the lock. Osric's apprentice Luth, who has been silent throughout, stands up suddenly. Three masked figures break in through the side window. They are not bandits. They are too well-equipped, too coordinated.

A short combat encounter follows: three intruders, mid-difficulty. The player and Luth fight them off. Osric does not fight — he stands over the forge, protecting the bell with his body. (This is an important characterization beat: Osric is old now, but he was once a fighter.)

After the fight, one of the intruders, dying, says only:

> *"The Compact... knows... the bell... should stay... buried..."*

He dies before naming names.

#### Scene 4 — Implication
Osric looks at the player.

> **Osric:** "Merchant Compact. Someone in the Compact knows what is beneath this town and wants it to stay there. I would have said that was sensible, an hour ago. Now I am not sure they have the town's good in mind. You go to the chapel. Take the bell. Tell Father Aldren what happened here. Tell him I will come if he calls for me, but not before — I have my own people to warn."

The player leaves the smithy. The Merchant Compact storyline now branches: depending on the player's standing with the Compact, they may already know who in the Compact is behind this, or it may be a mystery they investigate later. This is a planted hook for Hour 5 content and for post-main-quest faction work.

#### Reward
- 200 silver
- **Bellbinder's Voice** (quest-bound handbell, used in Q6, Q11, Q12)
- Crafting recipe: "Bell-Bronze Ingot" (a permanent unlock, usable in late-game crafting)
- "Forge-Apprentice Brand" cosmetic
- Town Watch reputation +100 (for surviving an attack)
- Merchant Compact reputation -100 (you are now on someone's watchlist)

### Q6: The Hidden Door
**Quest giver:** Father Aldren
**Level band:** Level 7+
**Type:** Combat + Investigation, 30-40 minutes

#### Scene 1 — The Chapel Cellar
Aldren is waiting in the chapel. He locks the chapel doors before descending with the player. Brother Vance has insisted on coming. The cellar is small, low-ceilinged, lit by a single oil lamp. At the far end of the cellar, behind a wine rack that has clearly been there for a hundred years, is a brick wall.

> **Aldren:** "Behind this brick is a door. I have never seen the door. I have only the floor plan. We are going to open the brick. Vance — sit there, please. Do not stand up unless I ask you to."

A short physical activity: the player swings a pick at the brick wall. After several swings, the brick gives way, revealing a heavy iron door. The door has no keyhole. It has, instead, a small bronze plate at chest height with a circular indent. The indent is the exact size of the rim of a handbell.

> **Aldren:** "Ring it. Just once."

The player rings the Bellbinder's Voice against the plate. The door grinds open. Cold air sighs out. Old air. Older than the chapel, older than the town. The smell is mineral, and somewhere underneath, faintly, the smell of blood that dried a long time ago.

#### Scene 2 — The Antechamber
The door opens onto a small stone antechamber. Eight columns, each carved with spiraling reliefs. The reliefs show, in sequence:

- A great wyrm flying over a river.
- A woman in robes ringing a bell.
- The wyrm landing on stone.
- The bell ringing.
- The wyrm falling asleep.
- The wyrm being buried.
- A chapel being built atop the burial.
- A town growing around the chapel.

This is the Bellbinders' founding mural. It is the first time anyone has read it in nine hundred years. Aldren reads aloud, in a voice that wants to break:

> **Aldren:** "Saint Verena, forgive us. We built our faith atop a pact. We have been ringing the binding for nine centuries and calling it worship."

Vance, sitting on the floor:

> **Vance:** "Don't apologize, Aldren. They prayed and meant it. The chapel has done good. The chapel can keep doing good. It just has to learn what it has been doing the whole time."

#### Scene 3 — The First Combat
At the far end of the antechamber, a smaller iron door is ajar. Beyond it, the player can hear something moving. The first wave of corrupted dead — long-dead pilgrims who came down here and were caught in the binding — shamble out.

A combat encounter: 4-6 low-level undead, designed to teach the player that the Underways have hostile inhabitants now. Aldren fights with the player, casting a healing aura that the player can stand in. Vance does not fight; he sits and prays, and his prayer provides a small damage buff against undead.

After the fight, the player can examine the bodies. They are wearing chapel pilgrim cloaks from a previous century. The most recent of them, judging by his clothing, died perhaps fifty years ago.

> **Aldren:** "They came down here. People I never knew were missing. The chapel has been losing people for centuries and writing them off as runaways. I owe each of them a funeral. After this. After all of this."

#### Scene 4 — The Threshold
The smaller iron door at the back of the antechamber opens onto a descending staircase. The staircase spirals down into darkness. The walls of the staircase are inscribed with bell-tones — a continuous helix of carved musical notation.

Aldren stops at the top of the stair.

> **Aldren:** "I cannot go further with you. I am needed in the town. If I am missing from chapel hours, people will notice, and a panic now is the worst thing that could happen. I will keep the door open. I will pray.
>
> "You go. Map the first hall. Come back. We will plan the next descent."

The player descends a few steps, looks back at Aldren — who is, suddenly and unexpectedly, smaller than he was when the quest began — and the screen fades to black.

The fade-to-black ends Hour Three.

#### Reward
- 250 silver
- "Bellbinder's Antechamber" lore unlock
- Chapel reputation +300
- Cosmetic: "Cellar-Dust Cloak" (a long, gray cloak with faded religious embroidery)
- Permanent access to the chapel undercroft and the first ring of the Underways

## II.6 HOUR FOUR — Into the Bellward

### Q7: The Bellward Halls
**Quest giver:** Father Aldren (continuation)
**Level band:** Level 8+
**Type:** Solo dungeon, 45-60 minutes

#### Design Intent
The first Underways ring — the Bellward Halls — is a solo-only zone. This is by design. The player needs to experience this hall alone, in candlelight, without a party to chatter over the ambient audio. The Bellward Halls are a tonal piece. They are also a controlled introduction to the Underways combat, navigation, and reading mechanics.

#### Layout
The Bellward Halls form a roughly circular ring around a central pillar, about 250 meters in diameter. The ring contains:

- **Six prayer chambers**, each dedicated to one of the other six Bellwards. Each chamber has a small altar, a relic, and a fragment of mural that depicts the dragon of the corresponding ward. (This is foreshadowing — the player is meeting Thaedryn's siblings before meeting Thaedryn.)
- **A bell-pattern floor** in the central hub. The floor is inlaid with bronze in the same bell pattern that cracked the bridge. The pattern is enormous from this angle.
- **The Listening Chamber**, a small alcove with a single stone bench and a circular hole in the floor through which a faint blue light glows. The hole leads further down.
- **Three combat encounters**, spread around the ring.
- **A boss encounter** in the central hub: **the First Choir**, a group of three corrupted Bellbinder spirits.

#### The Six Prayer Chambers (in order of discovery)

**Chamber of Aevith** — A relief of a small, sluggish river-wyrm asleep beneath a tower. The altar holds a single river stone with the word "First" carved into it. Reading it grants the player the lore entry *The Aevith Binding*. Ambient audio: slow, almost lazy water.

**Chamber of Karag-Drath** — A relief of an enormous mountain wyrm, scales of stone, asleep beneath a fortress. The altar holds a chip of black mountain glass. Lore entry: *The Stone Binding*. Ambient audio: deep, slow stone groans.

**Chamber of Vyrenia** — A relief of a long-winged sky wyrm, half-folded into a mountain peak. Altar: a single white feather, preserved under glass. Lore: *The Ash Binding*. Audio: high wind, distant.

**Chamber of Murvath** — A coiled sea wyrm beneath waves. Altar: a small spiral shell. Lore: *The Salt Binding*. Audio: faint surf.

**Chamber of Sylenne** — A wyrm whose body has become roots. Altar: a single live green leaf, somehow still alive after centuries. Lore: *The Green Binding*. Audio: faint forest creaking.

**Chamber of Korruthax** — A wyrm in molten rock. Altar: a small obsidian arrow. Lore: *The Fire Binding*. Audio: faint, distant crackling.

In each chamber, when the player reads the altar inscription, they hear, faintly, in the back of their audio mix, a voice that is *not* the well-voice. It is six different voices. The dragons. They are awake enough to feel the player passing. They are not threatening — yet. They are curious.

**Each chamber is also a future quest hub.** When the player completes the main quest and the dragons begin to stir continent-wide, the chambers will become travel points to the other six Bellwards.

#### Combat Encounters

**Encounter 1 — The Sleepwalkers**: 4-6 undead Bellbinders, low difficulty. They speak in fragments of liturgy as they fight: "ring it true... ring it true... ring it true..."

**Encounter 2 — The Failed Apprentice**: A single mid-difficulty miniboss, a more recent corpse than the others — perhaps 200 years old. He carries a broken handbell. After defeating him, the player can loot his broken bell, which becomes a lore item (*The Apprentice's Bell*) and a small +1 attunement bonus.

**Encounter 3 — The Listening Choir**: 6-8 weak undead surrounding a single chanting spirit in the Listening Chamber. The spirit will not fight. If the player kills the chanting spirit, the chamber falls silent. If the player kills the surrounding undead and *lets the spirit chant*, the chamber's blue light brightens and a hidden alcove opens — containing a quest item, the **Choir-Singer's Ring**, which provides +5% bell-damage in the boss fight.

This is a soft moral test: do you destroy or do you listen?

#### Boss — The First Choir
At the central pillar, three figures stand: an elderly woman in chapel robes, an old man in stonemason leathers, and a youth in apprentice's gray. All three are dead and have been for hundreds of years. They sing — three voices in close harmony — a hymn the player has never heard. The hymn is the Original Binding Song.

When the player approaches, the singing stops. The eldest figure speaks:

> **First Choir's Crone:** "You are not one of us. You are not robed. You are not silent. Why are you in our hall?"

The player has dialogue options:
- **Honest:** "I came down with the priest. The bell is broken. The town is in danger."
- **Aggressive:** Engage combat immediately.
- **Reverent:** Bow. Say nothing.

Each option leads to a different fight modifier:
- **Honest**: The Crone says, "Then you will need to prove your tongue. We sing. You must not fall." The fight begins. The Crone uses chant-based attacks the player must dodge while she sings; her health regenerates if her chant is uninterrupted for too long.
- **Aggressive**: All three engage immediately at full strength. Hardest version.
- **Reverent**: The youngest figure attacks alone, "to test you," while the elders watch. Easiest version. The elders then engage if the youngest falls.

In all three cases, the fight ends with the Crone speaking the same final line:

> **First Choir's Crone:** "Then go down. We could not finish the binding. We tried. We failed. We left a tomb half-built. We left our master alone with his sleep. We left the town to drift. If you can finish what we could not, go down and ring the True Bell. If you cannot, please — kill us all the way through, before you go."

The player can choose to formally end the Crone's existence (granting a small permanent passive: +3% damage in the Underways) or leave her standing (granting a Chapel reputation bonus).

#### Reward
- 400 silver
- Lore entries for all six other Bellwards
- "First Choir" boss-kill achievement
- Choice of two cosmetics: the Crone's veil, or the apprentice's gray hood
- Bell-attunement +1 (a hidden stat that affects bell-damage in the final boss)

### Q8: Voices in the Stone
**Quest giver:** Triggered automatically after Q7
**Level band:** Level 8+
**Type:** Lore + Choice, 30 minutes

#### Scene 1 — The Antechamber Stair
After the First Choir is dealt with, a hidden stair becomes visible in the Listening Chamber. It spirals tightly downward into a single stone room — Old Harth's Antechamber, the upper tier of his tomb.

#### Scene 2 — The Antechamber Itself
A small octagonal room. In the center, a stone sarcophagus with the simple inscription: HARTH OF AEVITHSFORD, BELLBINDER, LAST.

Above the sarcophagus, a stone slab is suspended from the ceiling by four iron chains. The slab is the **Memory Stone** — a Bellbinder relic. When the player approaches and touches it, the room dims, and Old Harth's voice fills the chamber.

#### Scene 3 — The Confession of Old Harth

The player hears, in their audio channel, a long monologue. This is voiced; the player should be invited to simply listen. It is the centerpiece of the entire lore. The text follows, in full, because it is the moment the entire main quest pivots:

> **Old Harth:** "If you are hearing me, then either you are a Bellbinder yourself — in which case, welcome, and forgive me for trapping you in this — or you are a stranger, and the bell has broken, and the town has begun to wake the thing beneath it. If it is the second, sit down. Listen to me. There is little time, and you must understand what you are doing before you do it.
>
> "My name was Harth. I was the last of the Bellbinders. I was old when I came to this valley and I am older now, in this stone, than I should be — Verena keep me, the binding holds my dreaming too.
>
> "There is a dragon beneath this floor. Her name is Thaedryn. She was the third of the seven we bound. She was, in my judgment, the *kindest* of the seven, which is not a word that fits a wyrm but is the right one for her. She had spent the previous century, before we caught her, herding river fish into shallows so they would not be eaten by the larger beasts of the deep. She was bored. She liked the sound of bells. That is how we caught her: she came when we rang.
>
> "I bound her, and I built this town atop her, and I rang the bell at dawn, midday, and dusk for forty years. When I was too old to ring it myself, I taught a boy, and the boy taught a girl, and the girl built a chapel where the bell could be rung by chapel hands. I told them all that the bell was just a bell. I told them this because if they had known the truth, they would have used it. They would have unbound her. They would have used her. The Bellbinders did not bind the dragons to *own* them. We bound them to give the world a thousand years of breath. To let men build cities. To let women bury their children old, not young. Do you understand?
>
> "I am telling you this because if the bell has broken, then someone, somewhere, has either failed in their duty or is plotting to break the binding on purpose. Both are possible. Find out which. If it is a failure, repair it: cast a new bell, restore the ringing, let Thaedryn keep sleeping, and the town will live another nine hundred years. If it is a plot, find the plotter and stop them. They will not understand what they are doing. They will think they are freeing something beautiful. They will be right. And it will eat them, and us, and the town, and the river, and the road, and the wheat, and everyone you have ever met, and it will not be cruel about it — it will just be hungry, the way a fire is not cruel when it burns a house.
>
> "There is a third path. I will tell you about it because you are owed all three. You can wake her. You can talk to her. She is intelligent. She is, in some ways, more intelligent than the priests who have served above her. If you wake her well — slowly, with respect, with the right words — she may not destroy the town. She may, if she chooses, fly away. She may, if she chooses, even *help*. She has six siblings, after all, and the others are not all as kind. The world that comes after the wyrms wake will need allies, and a Thaedryn who has been treated kindly may be one.
>
> "But this path is dangerous beyond what I can promise. She has been asleep for nine centuries. Her mind is not as it was. She has been *dreaming*, and her dreams have shaped her. She is angrier than she was. She is also lonelier. I do not know what waking her will produce. I will tell you only this: *if you choose to wake her, ring the bell once before you reach her chamber, not three times. Three times is the ending stroke. One time is a greeting.*
>
> "I am tired. I have spoken for too long. The stone will hold this voice for a few more telling, and then it will fall silent. Choose well. Choose with your friends. Choose for the town. And, if you choose to wake her — tell her, please, that Harth thought she was beautiful. I never told her. I should have."

The voice fades. The room is silent. The Memory Stone goes dark.

#### Scene 4 — The Player's First Real Choice
The player's quest log now offers three threads. Only one needs to be set, and it can be changed up until Q11. The threads are:

1. **Rebind.** Restore the ringing. Recast and rehang the bell. Thaedryn returns to sleep. The town survives untouched.
2. **Slay.** Descend, ring the ending stroke (three times), and kill Thaedryn in her dreaming form before she can fully wake. This is the easiest path, mechanically, but has the worst long-term political consequences.
3. **Wake.** Ring the greeting (one time), let her wake, and parley.

The player can talk to townsfolk about which path to choose. NPCs have opinions. (See the **Path Reactions Table** below.)

#### Path Reactions — Key NPC Stances

| NPC | Rebind | Slay | Wake |
|-----|--------|------|------|
| Father Aldren | Strongly supports | Strongly opposes | Reluctant; says "I will pray for you" |
| Mother Halene (via letters) | Was attempting this | Did not consider this | Did not consider this |
| Brother Vance | Supports cautiously | Strongly opposes | Strongly supports, calls it "kindness" |
| Master Osric Vale | Supports | Mixed | Opposes, says "Harth was a fool" |
| Nessa Crowe | Mixed | Opposes ("she's a *person*") | Supports |
| Caldus Merrow | Strongly supports | Supports | Strongly opposes |
| Tovin Reed | Supports | Mixed | Mixed |
| Edrik Vane | Strongly supports | Strongly supports | Opposes |
| Ysabet Fenlow | Supports | Opposes | Strongly supports |
| Sergeant Bram Holt | Supports | Strongly supports | Opposes |

#### Reward
- 500 silver
- "The Bellbinder's Confession" lore unlock
- Player's choice flag set (changeable until Q11)
- Cosmetic: "Harth's Memory" — a small ornate bronze pin

## II.7 HOUR FIVE — The Dragon's Veins

### Q9: The Veins of the Wyrm
**Quest giver:** Triggered after Q8
**Level band:** Level 9+
**Type:** Group dungeon (3-5 players, soloable at level +2), 60-75 minutes

#### Design Intent
The second Underways ring is where group play becomes critical. Players who have been progressing solo are gently nudged toward party-finder tools, guild recruitment, or open-world group formation. Mechanics here are designed for tank/healer/DPS roles, but the dungeon is scaled to also support 1-2 players with strong builds.

The "Veins" are channels of dragon essence — slow rivers of glowing, faintly amber-gold ichor that flow along carved channels in the walls and floor. These are the dragon's *aura*, leaking after centuries of dreaming.

#### Layout
A spiraling descending path through three large halls, connected by narrow stair tunnels. Each hall has a major encounter and several gathering nodes.

**Hall 1 — The Pulse Hall**: A circular chamber where the floor pulses faintly in time with the dragon's sleeping breath. Walls covered in carved veins of bronze that fill periodically with golden light. Encounter: **Vein-Beasts** — corrupted creatures that have fed on the leaking aura and become aggressive crystalline forms. Mid-tier combat.

**Hall 2 — The Echo Hall**: A vast hall with a single deep pool of dragon-essence at its center. Sound carries strangely here; the player can hear their own voice echo back at them, slightly delayed and slightly wrong. Encounter: **The Echo-Singer** — a miniboss, a spirit that mimics player abilities at half power. Mechanics-heavy: the boss casts a delayed version of the last spell each player used; players must learn not to telegraph their highest-damage ability.

**Hall 3 — The Spine Hall**: A long, narrow hall carved along what is, the player will eventually realize, an enormous bone — Thaedryn's spine. The walls of the hall are formed by her ribs. Encounter: **The Shed-Skin** — a wave-based fight against the dragon's molted scales, which are now animate and territorial. Mid-tier combat.

#### Boss — Vyrahel, the Vein-Keeper
At the end of the Spine Hall, the player meets Vyrahel, a small wyrmling — not Thaedryn's child, but a *fragment of her dreaming*. Vyrahel was born from her loneliness over the last few centuries: a tiny dragon, perhaps eight feet long, who patrols the veins like a watchdog. She is not malicious. She is desperate.

Vyrahel will speak to the player before fighting:

> **Vyrahel:** "Mother sleeps. You should not be here. You smell like bell. You smell like the cold-bell-people. Are you here to ring her? Don't ring her. She's tired."

The player has three dialogue options:
- "We're here to help her sleep again." → Vyrahel attacks, sad but determined.
- "We're here to wake her gently." → Vyrahel hesitates. "...gently?" She still attacks, but at reduced aggression, and may be parried into a non-lethal defeat. If non-lethally defeated, she survives and becomes a permanent pet/companion NPC in the post-quest content.
- "We're here to kill her." → Vyrahel attacks at full strength, weeping audibly. Hardest version.

The fight is a single-boss encounter with three phases. Vyrahel is small, fast, and uses minor breath attacks. Phase 2 introduces vein-tendrils that grow from the walls. Phase 3 is a brief burrowing pattern.

#### Reward
- 750 silver
- "Vein-Keeper's Scale" — a crafting material for late-game armor
- If Vyrahel was spared non-lethally: she appears as an NPC in the town post-quest, perched on the chapel roof, available for short rumor dialogues
- Group reputation bonus across all faction tracks

### Q10: The Bellbinder's Tomb
**Quest giver:** Triggered after Q9
**Level band:** Level 9+
**Type:** Group dungeon (3-5 players), 45-60 minutes

#### Scene 1 — Old Harth's Sealed Tomb
After Vyrahel falls, a passage opens that leads to Old Harth's true tomb — not the antechamber from Q8, but the burial chamber itself, deeper down. The door is sealed with a complex bronze mechanism shaped like a bell-clapper.

To open it, the players must perform a brief group ritual: each player rings a smaller bell at one of four positions around the door. The bells must be rung in sequence. (This is the dungeon's first explicit group-coordination puzzle.) If the players are running solo at higher level, they can substitute by ringing each bell themselves with a short cooldown between rings.

#### Scene 2 — The Tomb
The tomb is a single large chamber. At its center: Old Harth's sarcophagus. Around it, six smaller plinths, each holding a piece of Bellbinder regalia — a stole, a hammer, a tuning fork, a small bronze handbell, a chain, and a ring.

When the player approaches Harth's sarcophagus, a final hostile spirit rises from it: not Harth himself, but **the Tomb's Guardian — Kelvin Voss**, Harth's first apprentice, who died sealing the tomb and whose spirit has guarded it since.

#### Scene 3 — The Guardian's Argument
Kelvin Voss does not attack immediately. He speaks first.

> **Kelvin:** "Stop. *Stop.* I know what you are. You are a fellow descendant of the work. I have stood here for nine hundred years to keep this tomb shut. Do not open the sarcophagus. Do not touch the regalia. Tell me what you have come to do."

The player declares their path (Rebind / Slay / Wake). Kelvin's reaction:
- **Rebind**: He nods. "Then take the regalia. You will need it. I will give you my key. But I will fight you for the courtesy of it." He fights at moderate difficulty.
- **Slay**: He fights at full difficulty and refuses to discuss further.
- **Wake**: He weeps — a strange thing to see a ghost do — and says, "Then take the regalia. Take the key. Take all of it. I have wanted, for nine centuries, to ask her forgiveness. Tell her, please, that her cage-keeper is sorry." He fights at the lowest difficulty, almost ceremonially.

#### Scene 4 — The Boss Fight
Kelvin's fight is mechanically complex regardless of path. He has three phases:
- **Phase 1**: He casts the same untuning spells the Bellbinders once used on Thaedryn, now turned against the players. The players must interrupt his chant.
- **Phase 2**: He summons the six pieces of regalia animated as floating objects, each with different attacks.
- **Phase 3**: He fuses with the sarcophagus itself, becoming a stone-and-bronze giant. The players must break specific weak points.

#### Scene 5 — The Regalia
On defeating Kelvin, the player can claim the six regalia pieces. These are critical for Q11. They can be distributed across a party (one per role) or all held by one player.

The regalia:

| Piece | Function in Q11/Q12 |
|-------|---------------------|
| The Stole | Allows the wearer to read Bellbinder script and speak Old Tongue commands |
| The Hammer | Used to strike the True Bell in Q11; high damage in Q12 |
| The Tuning Fork | Sets the bell-tone for Q11; provides a permanent allied buff in Q12 |
| The Handbell | The "greeting bell" — used to ring the *one* tone if waking Thaedryn |
| The Chain | Used to physically secure or release the True Bell, mechanically |
| The Ring | The "binding seal" — used to formally close the binding if rebinding |

#### Reward
- 1000 silver
- Six Bellbinder Regalia pieces (quest-bound)
- "Tomb-Keeper's Boon" — passive bonus in Q12
- Cosmetic: the Bellbinder's robe (long, gray-bronze, embroidered with bell-spiral motifs)
- Achievement: "Last Apprentice Laid to Rest"

#### Scene 6 — Aftermath
Old Harth's sarcophagus is now openable. Inside lies a perfectly preserved skeleton wearing a small bronze medallion. The medallion is the **Founder's Seal**. The player can take it. It is the final key. With it, they can now access the deepest chamber — the Wyrm's Bed — where Thaedryn sleeps.

The dungeon exit returns the player to the chapel undercroft. Hour Five ends. Aldren is waiting. Caldus Merrow is also waiting. Word has spread, somehow, that someone is below.

The town is now in a tense state. Public dialogue lines from townfolk reflect anxiety. The market is quieter. The bell-tower bell does not ring anymore (it has been replaced by a wooden clapper to maintain the rhythm of the day, but the sound is wrong). Players in the town will hear NPCs whisper about it.

## II.8 HOUR SIX — The Bellbound

### Q11: The Last Ringing
**Quest giver:** Father Aldren (or, if Wake path: a brief vision of Thaedryn herself)
**Level band:** Level 10+
**Type:** Race-against-time group quest (3-5 players), 35-45 minutes

#### Scene 1 — The Decision Made
The player formally confirms their chosen path with Aldren and Caldus on the chapel grounds. The two of them have argued. They have been arguing for the entire time the player was in the tomb. Caldus is, surprisingly, leaning toward Rebind. Aldren is leaning toward Wake — a reversal from Q8.

Whichever the player chooses, both NPCs accept it and provide closing dialogue:

> **Aldren (if Rebind):** "Then go and silence her properly. Verena keep you. I will ring the chapel bell-stub at the proper hours until you return."
>
> **Aldren (if Slay):** "Then I will pray for the souls of the dead. Hers among them. Go."
>
> **Aldren (if Wake):** "Then I will be the first chapel in nine hundred years to receive a wyrm. May Verena forgive me. May Verena forgive *me*. Go."

#### Scene 2 — The Descent
The party descends through the Underways to the Wyrm's Bed antechamber. This is a final pre-boss zone: a long bronze corridor where the dragon's breathing is now audible. Slow, deep, vast.

Three small encounters happen during the descent:

1. **Compact Saboteurs** — A group of three Merchant Compact agents (the same faction that attacked the smithy in Q5) try to ambush the party. They are trying to prevent any outcome except their own. They will not say what their outcome is until defeated. After the fight, one survivor reveals: "The Compact has bought the dragon's bones already. We have a buyer. You are ruining our plan." This is the player's first explicit confirmation that the Merchant Compact storyline is connected.

2. **The Bell-Mad** — A wave of corrupted townsfolk — people who heard the bell too clearly over the years and went mad. They are not enemies in the usual sense; they are tragedies. The player can fight them or use the Stole's Old Tongue to send them to peaceful death.

3. **The Threshold Wyrm-Voice** — A non-combat encounter. The dragon's voice speaks to the party through the corridor walls. She knows they are coming. She speaks each player's name aloud. (This is harvested from player character names — a small but powerful technical effect.) Her tone differs by path:
   - Rebind/Slay: cold, sorrowful, resigned.
   - Wake: curious, almost playful, deeply tired.

#### Scene 3 — The Bell Chamber
The party reaches the Bellward Chamber — the room directly above the Wyrm's Bed. This is where the True Bell hangs. The True Bell is enormous: forty feet tall, cast in dark bronze, suspended from chains thicker than a man's body. The bell's clapper is the size of a small house.

#### Scene 4 — Path-Specific Mechanics

**REBIND PATH:**
The party must repair and re-strike the bell at the proper tone. This requires:
- One player wielding the Hammer (high physical stat).
- One player using the Tuning Fork (high focus/casting stat).
- One player using the Chain to lower the bell into position.
- One player using the Stole to chant the Old Tongue binding.
- One player (optional, if a fifth) using the Ring to formally seal.

Simultaneously, waves of corrupted essence-creatures attack the chamber. The party must defend while performing the ritual. There are three full ring-cycles. If two of the three succeed, the binding is restored.

**SLAY PATH:**
The party rings the bell *three times in rapid succession* — the ending stroke. This is intended to kill Thaedryn outright before she can wake. It is mechanically the simplest path: three rings, three defended-phases. But the floor begins to crack on the third ring. Thaedryn does not die quietly.

**WAKE PATH:**
The party rings the Handbell — the small greeting bell — *once*. The True Bell is left untouched. Thaedryn wakes slowly, in stages. The ground begins to shake. The party must descend further to meet her properly.

In all three paths, the floor of the Bellward Chamber eventually breaks open. The party falls — controlled cinematic fall — into the Wyrm's Bed itself.

#### Reward
- 1500 silver
- Final-path-confirmation flag
- Pre-boss cosmetic: "Bellward Robe" (gray with the player's chosen path's symbol)
- Full party buff: "Bell-Tuned" — +10% all stats for the boss fight

### Q12: Thaedryn the Bellbound
**Quest giver:** Final boss encounter
**Level band:** Level 10+
**Type:** Climactic boss fight, 30-45 minutes
**Encounter type:** 3-5 player group; soloable only at significantly higher level

#### The Wyrm's Bed
The party falls into a chamber the size of the town above them. The ceiling is vaulted natural stone, the walls are carved with thousands of years of bell-spiral murals, and at the chamber's center, curled around herself like an enormous coiled river, is Thaedryn.

She is huge. From snout to tail, she is roughly 200 feet long. Her scales are the color of old bronze with patches of pale river-stone gray. Her wings are folded against her back; the wing membranes are translucent in places, like vellum held to candlelight. Her eyes — when they open — are the color of low candle-flame, gold-orange, with vertical pupils.

She is beautiful. The player should feel this. The art and animation budget should reflect it.

#### Boss Design — Universal Mechanics
Regardless of path, the boss fight has a shared structure:

- **Phase 1 — The Sleeper**: Thaedryn fights from a semi-sleeping posture. Slow, sweeping tail attacks. Breath weapon that is a *cloud of sound* — a localized untuning effect that interrupts player casting in zones. Vulnerability: the four chains anchoring her to the chamber floor.

- **Phase 2 — The Half-Waking**: Two chains broken. She lifts her head. Her movements become quicker. Breath weapon adds a physical river-water component (force damage in cones).

- **Phase 3 — The Bellbound**: Three chains broken. She is on her feet. She uses her wings to gust the party. Adds: small vein-beast minions.

- **Phase 4 — Path-Dependent**: All four chains broken or unbreaking.

#### Phase 4 by Path

**REBIND PATH (Phase 4):**
The bell from above falls into the chamber as scripted. Players must reposition it and ring it to weaken Thaedryn enough to re-bind. She fights at peak strength for ~3 minutes. Then a final coordinated ring-cycle puts her back into a deeper, more secure sleep. She does not die. The bell sings continuously. She closes her eyes. The party walks out.

**SLAY PATH (Phase 4):**
Players burn down her health bar without a script-event. She fights at peak strength, with desperate aggression. Toward 10% health, she stops attacking and speaks:

> **Thaedryn:** "I was kind to your kind. I was sleeping. I would have slept another thousand years. You should have let me. Tell — Harth — *(she gasps)* — tell him —"

She dies mid-sentence. Her body collapses. The chamber falls silent. Players loot her body. A massive XP reward and unique drops. The town is safe. Forever, by this method. But the world above is also poorer for it.

**WAKE PATH (Phase 4):**
After three chains break, players must *stop attacking*. If any player continues to attack past a threshold, the path collapses into Slay. If the party holds attack and instead uses the Handbell or the Stole's Old Tongue, Thaedryn slows. Her eyes meet a player's eyes. She speaks:

> **Thaedryn:** "You did not strike the third ring. You... rang the greeting. After nine hundred years, you rang the greeting. Are you a Bellbinder? You are not robed. You smell of forge and worry. Who are you?"

A brief negotiation dialogue follows. The player can:
- Apologize for the binding.
- Ask for her help.
- Ask her to leave peacefully.
- Tell her about her siblings.

Thaedryn's reaction depends on the player's history (rep, prior dialogue choices). If the player has been compassionate throughout the quest, she becomes a tentative ally. She breaks the remaining chain herself. She rises through the ceiling — through the chapel, through the bell tower, *through the town* — and emerges into the sky above Harthmere. She circles once. She speaks to the entire town in a voice everyone hears:

> **Thaedryn:** "I have been your shadow. I will be your shadow no longer. I owe you a debt for the gentleness of my waking. I will repay it when I can. My siblings are waking too. They will not be as gentle. *Be ready.*"

She flies west, into the mountains, and is gone.

#### Universal Aftermath Cinematic

Regardless of path, the final cinematic takes the player back to the surface — emerging through a new opening in the chapel floor, or, in the Wake case, through the broken chapel. The town is gathered. Aldren is there. Caldus is there. Nessa is there. Osric is there. Everyone the player has spoken to is there.

The reactions differ by path:
- **Rebind**: Quiet relief. The town slowly returns to normal. The bell rings again. Aldren weeps with gratitude.
- **Slay**: Silence. The town will heal, but the chapel's authority is broken. Aldren resigns. A new priest will be appointed. The player is a hero with no celebration.
- **Wake**: Awe. Terror. Joy. The town is changed. The chapel becomes a different kind of place. Aldren stays. He says, "We have a new duty now."

In all three paths, the final shot is the same: night falls over Harthmere. The camera lifts away from the town, up over the bridge, up over the wilderness, up into the high air over the continent. The camera turns, and we see — far in the distance, on six different horizons — six lights. The six other Bellwards. Five of them are calm. One of them is burning.

The text appears on screen:

> *"The Wyrm Age was a thousand years.
> The Bellward Age was nine hundred.
> The third age has begun."*

The credits-style sequence rolls the player's achievements during the main quest, then returns to gameplay.

#### Final Rewards (path-dependent)

**Rebind Path:**
- 5000 silver
- "The Hand That Steadies the Bell" — Legendary title
- "Re-Bound" set (chest, helm, gloves) — gray-bronze, religious aesthetic
- Mount: a tame river horse with bronze barding
- Permanent town buff: Aldren's blessing (+5% XP in Harthmere region)

**Slay Path:**
- 7500 silver (highest gold reward — the dragon's hoard)
- "Wyrmslayer" — Legendary title
- "Dragon-Bone" weapon (player's class) — visually carved from Thaedryn's body
- Mount: an armored warhorse
- Negative reputation with Chapel Circle (significant), positive with Town Watch (significant)
- Permanent town buff: civic gratitude (+10% repair discount in Harthmere)

**Wake Path:**
- 3000 silver (lowest gold — but other rewards)
- "She-Who-Was-Greeted" — Mythic title
- "Bellbinder's Successor" robe set (full set, glowing bronze trim)
- Pet/Companion: Vyrahel (if spared in Q9) appears as a permanent flying companion
- Continent-wide perk: Thaedryn occasionally appears in the sky over later content, providing a brief area buff
- Locked-out content: Some Town Watch quests now refuse you

## II.9 The Post-Main-Quest State of Harthmere

Once the main quest is complete, the town visibly changes for the player. Several persistent state changes occur:

| State Change | Rebind | Slay | Wake |
|--------------|--------|------|------|
| Chapel bell rings | Yes, new bell | No (replaced with wood clapper) | No (chapel partly broken open) |
| Father Aldren in chapel | Yes | No (replaced by Sister Maelle) | Yes, but transformed |
| Old Well sealed | Tightly | Iron-capped | Open as memorial |
| Underways entrance | Sealed | Sealed | Open as guided tour |
| Thaedryn in sky | Never | Never | Occasional flyovers |
| Vyrahel in town | If spared | Dies | Permanent (chapel roof) |
| Merchant Compact storyline | Active | Active | Active |

The Merchant Compact subplot remains unresolved regardless of path. The Compact buyer who tried to purchase Thaedryn's bones is still active and now must be pursued. This is the first piece of post-main-quest content.

The other six Bellwards become available as expansion zones, each with its own dragon, its own bound town, and its own equivalent main quest. The player has, by completing Harthmere's quest, established themselves as either:
- A Bellbinder (Rebind path)
- A Wyrmslayer (Slay path)
- A Wyrmspeaker (Wake path)

Each title affects how the other six Bellwards greet the player.

## III.2 The Town Watch

### Sergeant Bramwell "Bram" Holt
**Race:** Human (Riverlander)
**Age:** 47
**Family:** Widower; one daughter (Yenna, age 12, chronically ill); one elder brother (Garin, a farmer twenty miles upriver)
**Faction:** Town Watch (Sergeant rank)
**Residence:** Guard barracks, room above the Guard Yard
**Daily routine:** Up at dawn for shift change; spends mornings at the North Gate; afternoons at Guard Yard reviewing patrols; evenings with his daughter at the apothecary or her sickroom; bed by midnight

**Backstory:** Bram was born in a farming village a day's ride upriver. He came to Harthmere at sixteen as a recruit for the Town Watch, married a baker's daughter named Anwen at twenty-four, and lost her to fever the same winter their daughter was born. He has been raising Yenna alone for the last decade with the help of half the town. Yenna's chronic illness — a wasting disease of the joints that the apothecary suspects is congenital — has been the central fact of his adult life. He has bribed, lied, and stretched his salary for the better part of ten years to pay for her treatments. He has never stolen. He has never taken a bribe larger than enough to cover one month's medicine. He has never told a soul.

**Personality:** Stern, practical, deeply tired. Has a dry sense of humor that surfaces with people he trusts. Slow to anger; almost impossible to provoke. Treats new recruits with the patience of a man who has seen too many funerals. Will sometimes stare at children for slightly too long in a way that is not creepy but is heartbreaking.

**Voice/speech style:** Short sentences. Uses the player's title or surname rather than first names until trust is earned. Calls everyone "friend" once they've earned it. Has a habit of looking at the river before answering hard questions.

**Secrets:**
- Takes small bribes for Yenna's medicine. Knows it is wrong. Has not stopped.
- Knows the Reeve has noticed and has chosen not to mention it.
- Has a personal ledger of every bribe taken, every favor granted, every name involved. Hidden in the Guard barracks beneath his bunk. Plans to confess publicly when Yenna no longer needs medicine — or when she dies.
- Believes, privately, that the Reeve is a more dangerous man than the town realizes.

**Quest hooks:**
- The bridge-cracks investigation (Q1)
- Caravan defense dailies
- A long side-quest chain about Yenna's illness (see Part IV)
- A potential late-game arc where Bram's ledger is discovered and the player must choose whether to expose him or help him hide it

## III.5 The Chapel and Faith

### Father Aldren Mell
**Race:** Half-elven (one human parent, one half-elven parent — Aldren is approximately one-quarter elven by blood)
**Age:** 53 (looks 43)
**Family:** Single by chapel vow; biological family in the southern bishopric, rarely visits
**Faction:** Chapel Circle (leader)
**Residence:** Small chamber attached to the chapel
**Daily routine:** Dawn prayer; chapel hours from 7 AM; mid-morning private study; afternoon visits to the sick and the dying; evening prayer; bed by 10 PM, but rarely sleeps well

**Backstory:** Aldren was sent to chapel school at age 12, ordained at 24, and posted to Harthmere at 29. He has served here for 24 years. He was Mother Halene's junior, then her sole co-priest, then her successor. He inherited the chapel's secret half-knowing and has spent two and a half years failing to fully reckon with it.

He is profoundly kind. He has, by his own quiet count, presided over 487 funerals, 1,128 baptisms, 312 marriages, and tens of thousands of services. He knows his town. He loves his town. He believes Saint Verena has called him here for a reason.

He is also, increasingly, in a private crisis of faith. The dragon beneath the chapel is, for him, a theological earthquake. If the chapel was built atop a binding — if the faith has been, in some sense, a tool of a pre-Verenine pact — then what is faith? He has not yet found his answer.

**Personality:** Gentle, burdened, profoundly steady in public. In private, with people he trusts, he is funnier and more questioning than people expect. Loves to laugh at himself. Quotes scripture and then immediately undercuts it with a joke.

**Voice/speech style:** Warm, careful. Uses old scriptural phrasing without irony but also without sanctimony. Often pauses to listen.

**Secrets:**
- Has known the rough shape of the chapel's secret for three years. Has carried it alone.
- Was briefly in love, twenty years ago, with a woman named Eira who lived in a southern parish. She married another man. Aldren has never quite stopped thinking about her. He does not consider this a failing.
- Has, on two occasions, knowingly forgiven sins he believed he should not have forgiven — including, once, a man who had murdered his wife. The man came to him broken with guilt. Aldren absolved him. Aldren is still not sure whether this was right.

**Quest hooks:**
- The entire main quest revolves around him
- Healing and blessing services
- Funeral procession events
- A long, slow arc about his crisis of faith and his eventual reconciliation with what the chapel has been

# PART IV — EXPANDED SIDE QUEST CATALOG

This section provides side quests organized by NPC giver. Each entry includes premise, beats, choice branches, and rewards. These quests are designed to be **independent of the main Bellbound arc** so players can pursue them at their own pace, but several connect tangentially — completing a particular side quest may grant a small advantage during the main quest's climax (these connections are noted as **"Bell-Tie"** markers where they exist).

Quests are tagged with **level bands**, **time estimates**, **content type** (solo / small group / public), and **reputation impact**.

---

## IV.1 — SERGEANT BRAM HOLT (North Gate)

### SQ-001: The Gate Ledger
**Level band:** 1–4 | **Time:** 20 min | **Solo** | **Watch +50**

**Premise:** A merchant cart was waved through yesterday without paying toll. Bram suspects one of his junior guards is taking bribes.

**Beats:**
1. Speak to the toll clerk — get the ledger
2. Cross-reference the ledger with the merchant's actual cargo manifest (found at the docks)
3. Identify the discrepancy
4. Confront the guard (a young man named Tessen Hark) or report him to Bram

**Choices:**
- **Report him formally:** Tessen is demoted. Bram thanks you. Watch +50, but you'll see Tessen in Mudden Ward later, bitter.
- **Give him a chance:** He returns the bribe. Bram never knows. Tessen becomes a friendly face. Watch +25.
- **Take the bribe yourself:** Watch -100, but you gain a contact who will warn you about future inspections.

**Rewards:** 15 silver, "Gate Ledger" item (used in two later quests), choice of Watch tabard pin OR merchant favor token.

---

### SQ-002: The Vidda's Daughter
**Level band:** 3–6 | **Time:** 35 min | **Solo** | **Watch +75, personal**

**Premise:** Bram's daughter Mira (age 8) has fallen ill with marsh fever. The chapel will treat her for free, but the apothecary's stronger medicine costs more than Bram can afford on his salary.

**Beats:**
1. Bram, drunk for the first time the player has ever seen, lets this slip
2. Player can pursue several paths to help
3. Return with the medicine OR an alternative

**Choices:**
- **Pay Ysabet directly:** 40 silver out of pocket. Bram is grateful.
- **Earn the money through legitimate work:** Series of small jobs around town.
- **Convince Caldus Merrow to grant Bram a hardship loan:** Requires Noble Rise approach.
- **Steal the medicine from Ysabet:** Possible but Ysabet eventually traces it. Apothecary -150.
- **Confront Bram about his bribe-taking and offer him a way out:** Surprisingly emotional scene.

**Rewards:** "Bram's Token" — a small Watch sergeant pin Bram pressed into the player's hand. In any later quest involving the Watch, this grants +1 dialogue option.

**Bell-Tie:** Carrying Bram's Token during Q12 unlocks unique dialogue if Walt Ormsby is in the boss fight — Walt recognizes the pin and acknowledges the player as someone Bram trusted.

---

### SQ-003: Strangers at the Gate
**Level band:** 5–8 | **Time:** 25 min | **Solo** | **Watch +50, Chapel +25**

**Premise:** A family of three has been camping outside the gate for two days. They have no papers, no money, and refuse to speak with anyone but a priest. Bram cannot legally let them in without identification.

**Beats:**
1. Speak to the family — they're refugees from a village south of Pellmarra, fleeing a "shadow on the well"
2. Speak with Father Aldren about chapel sanctuary
3. Convince Caldus Merrow or Bram to bend the rules

**Choices:**
- **Chapel sanctuary:** Aldren takes them in. Chapel +50, Watch +25.
- **Bram bends rules:** Bram registers them under "religious supplicants" — technically legal. Watch +50.
- **Smuggle them in via Mudden Ward:** Mudden Kin +75, Watch -25.
- **Turn them away:** They leave. Three weeks later, a courier from Pellmarra arrives asking after them. The player feels bad.

**Rewards:** 25 silver, faction reputation per choice, and a long-term contact in Pellmarra (the father, Aerek, sends a letter six months later).

**Worldbuilding seed:** "Shadow on the well" is — though the player won't know this for some time — an early sign of Pellmarra's Bellbinder lattice failing. Pellmarra holds the dragon Murvath. This is the game's first hint that Harthmere is not the only Bellward with problems.

### SQ-005: Patrol Route Seven
**Level band:** 4–7 | **Time:** 40 min | **Solo or duo** | **Watch +75**

**Premise:** A patrol route along the river road has been "going dark" — guards return claiming they saw nothing, but villagers along the route report strange lights. Walt asks the player to walk Route Seven that night.

**Beats:**

### SQ-010: Three Casks Short
**Level band:** 2–4 | **Time:** 25 min | **Solo** | **Inn +50**

# PART V — RELIGION, MAGIC, AND CLASS IDENTITY

This section fills in the spiritual, magical, and identity-shaping systems that make Harthmere's world feel coherent. The town's design hinges on Saint Verena's chapel and the Old Pact, but the Bellbinders' magic is its own peculiar thing — neither cleric nor sorcerer. Players need to know how all of this *feels* even if they never directly play a Bellbinder class.

---

## V.1 — THE VERENINE FAITH

### Core Tenets

The Chapel Circle worships **Saint Verena of the Listening Water**. Verena was, in canonical lore, a mortal woman who lived roughly four hundred years ago. She is not a goddess but a saint — a mortal whose deeds and visions earned her veneration. The Verenine faith is not a religion of an omnipotent creator; it is a religion of *attention*. The central act of worship is **listening**.

The four cardinal virtues of the faith are:

| Virtue | Meaning | Practical Expression |
|---|---|---|
| **Listening** | Attention to the world, to the dead, to the silent. | Vigils, candle-watching, dawn prayer. |
| **Tending** | Care for what cannot care for itself. | Charity, grave-keeping, refugee aid. |
| **Bearing** | The carrying of burdens that should not be set down. | Confession, vows, the keeping of secrets. |
| **Naming** | The right and sacred act of speaking true names. | Funeral rites, child-blessings, the marking of evil. |

### The Saint Herself

Verena is said to have lived along a river not far from Harthmere. As a child she heard voices in water — the dead, the unborn, the never-quite-born. She was institutionalized, escaped, became a wandering listener. She advised farmers, priests, and finally a king. She was killed by a noble who feared her counsel, drowned in the same river that had spoken to her.

Her body was never recovered. The faith holds that she did not die — she "stepped through" into the water and listens still.

### Relationship to the Bell

Here is where the canonical faith and the Bellbinders intersect: the Verenine faith taught that bells were a way to *concentrate listening*. A bell is silent until rung; in the moment of ringing, every ear becomes a single ear. The Bellbinders, four hundred years later, used this principle to bind the dragons.

To the Verenine clergy, the bells that bind the dragons are *sacred listening-instruments turned to hard purpose*. The clergy do not entirely approve — many Verenine theologians considered the binding an act of moral compromise — but they tend the bells nonetheless. This is the source of Father Aldren's guilt: he knows that what was done was, in some sense, against his own faith.

### Chapel Services in Game

| Service | What It Does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Blessing | Small temporary buff (15 min) | 5 silver or charity donation |
| Cleansing | Removes corruption/disease debuffs | 20 silver |
| Resurrection | Returns dead player to chapel | Free (gold-sink penalty on revive) |
| Naming Rite | Sets player's "true name" — used in certain quests | Free, once |
| Vigil | Long buff (24 hr in-game) if player meditates at altar | Free |
| Confession | NPC interaction — confess sins to Father Aldren | Lore, reputation changes |

## V.2 — OTHER FAITHS PRESENT IN HARTHMERE

### The Old Riverkeepers

A pre-Verenine folk religion still practiced quietly in the Mudden Ward and among the river folk. They worship the river itself — not Saint Verena, but the Veyne as a being. They tie blue ribbons. They float candles. They believe drowned souls become river-spirits, and that the river protects those who treat it with respect.

The Verenine clergy *tolerate* the Riverkeepers; some, like Sister Maelle, consider them sister-faiths.

### The Compact Faith

A practical, almost civic, religion practiced by the wealthier merchants. They venerate **Marvain the Measured**, a minor figure said to have invented honest weights and measures. There is a small shrine on Noble Rise. The faith is more social club than theology, but its tenets — fair dealing, honest contracts, the sanctity of the spoken bargain — are taken surprisingly seriously by its adherents.

### The Mudden Ward Mysteries

A loose collection of folk practices — wards against the evil eye, charms against drowning, household gods of hearth and hinge. Not a religion per se, but a vast network of small rituals that any Mudden Ward resident can recite.

### Distant Faiths

Travelers and refugees bring fragments of other faiths — sun-cults from the southern kingdoms, ancestor-veneration from the Northborn clans, dwarven hearth-rites, half-elven naming-cycles. The chapel does not police belief. Aldren keeps a small "guest shrine" in a chapel alcove where any traveler may light a candle to their own god.

## V.3 — MAGIC IN HARTHMERE

Magic in this setting is **rare**, **expensive**, and **slow**. It is not a daily tool. It is closer to surgery than to programming — a specialized discipline that takes decades to master and whose practitioners are few.

### The Sources of Magic

There are, by the world's understanding, four "wells" from which magic is drawn:

| Source | Nature | Practitioners |
|---|---|---|
| **The Listening (Verenine)** | Magic of attention, blessing, sanctification. | Clergy, especially senior priests. |
| **The Old Wood (Druidic)** | Magic of growth, root, animal-speech, weather. | Druids, the Moss-Woman, some halflings. |
| **The Stone (Bellbinder)** | Magic of tone, vibration, binding. | The Seven Bellbinders (extinct or near-extinct). |
| **The Self (Sorcerous)** | Magic of will, fire, force — drawn from the practitioner's own life. | Rare. Often unstable. |

### What Magic Cannot Do

The setting is deliberately low-magic by MMO standards. Magic in Harthmere:

- Cannot raise the dead (except by Bellbinder, which is a different kind of working)
- Cannot teleport (long-distance travel requires roads, rivers, or — in the future — controlled portals)
- Cannot create matter from nothing
- Cannot read minds (only emotional states, and only with effort)
- Cannot grant immortality (Veneth's longevity is racial, not magical)

### What Magic Can Do

- Heal (slowly, with strain on the caster)
- Bless and curse
- Speak with animals (druidic)
- Speak with the dead (Verenine, rarely, briefly)
- Bind objects, places, or beings (Bellbinder)
- Sense corruption or sanctity
- Create small lights, fires, sounds
- Reinforce or weaken bonds (literal and metaphorical)

### Class Fantasy and Magic

Player classes in Harthmere fall along a spectrum. The game does not assume a "wizard college" — those exist far away. Most players will be warriors, rangers, rogues, hunters, or modestly-trained priests. Pure casters are rare and powerful and slow.

## V.4 — CLASS FANTASIES IN THE BELLBOUND ARC

How does each major class fantasy *experience* the main quest? The quest is class-agnostic in mechanics, but the narrative grants different roles spotlight at different points.

### Warrior / Fighter / Knight

**Q1–Q3:** Standard. Combat appropriate.
**Q4 (Voices in the Stone):** Walt Ormsby specifically acknowledges the player's martial skill. "Good. We'll need you with us."
**Q9–Q10:** Lead role in tank/protect mechanics. Bram or Walt offers personal commendations.
**Q12:** Frontline against Thaedryn. Unique dialogue: "He'll come for you first. Stand."

### Ranger / Hunter / Tracker

**Q1–Q3:** Spotlight in investigative tracking moments.
**Q2 (Whispers at the Well):** Tracking the strange footprints around the well grants ranger unique dialogue with Selka Doryn — Selka teaches the player a tracking technique passed down from the Old Hunter.
**Q9:** The Veins of the Wyrm dungeon — rangers can spot Thaedryn's "scale-shed" deposits earlier than other classes, granting a bonus relic.
**Q12:** Unique flanking position. Tovin Reed comments on the player's "good eyes for the kill."

### Rogue / Scout / Knife

**Q2.5 (The Rat-Girl Knows):** This quest is functionally a rogue-favored experience. Nessa Crowe gives the rogue much more useful information.
**Q5 (Beneath the Stones):** Rogues can find an alternative entry to the Bellward Halls that bypasses one mini-boss.
**Q12:** Unique positioning option to attempt to wound Thaedryn's "binding tongue" — a high-risk, high-reward move only available to stealth classes. Successful execution grants a unique post-fight title and shortens the fight by a phase.

### Cleric / Priest / Acolyte

**Q1–Q12:** This is, narratively, the cleric's quest. Aldren takes special interest in any cleric player. Maelle Frenn confides in clerics earlier than other classes. The Naming Rite is more potent.
**Q11:** A cleric player can — uniquely — perform the formal Verenine Listening Rite over Thaedryn, which adds a moral dimension to the Slay path that other classes don't experience. Clerics who choose Slay must reckon with what they've done in a way other classes don't.
**Q12:** A cleric who chooses Rebind co-leads the ritual with Aldren and is forever marked as one of the Bellbinders' successors. Major late-game implication.

### Druid / Wildmage / Naturist

**Q3 (Dreams of Drowning):** Druids have unique dream-sequence interactions — they recognize Thaedryn's presence as a *living thing* rather than a monster, earlier than other classes.
**Q10 (The Bellbinder's Tomb):** A druid player can sense Veneth the Moss-Woman's old presence in the tomb. Unique dialogue.
**Q11:** Druids can speak directly with Thaedryn during the boss approach. They are the only class who can attempt the **Wake** path's "Free Passage" outcome solo (other classes need Veneth's help).
**Q12 (Wake path):** Druid players can convince Thaedryn to bond with the player as a wandering companion (a *very* rare endgame outcome).

### Mage / Sorcerer / Scholar

**Q4 (Voices in the Stone):** The pivotal scene where Halpen Wren describes the binding rune is dramatically different for mages — they can *read* parts of the inscription the others cannot.
**Q5 (Beneath the Stones):** Mages can identify the tomb's wardings and bypass two traps.
**Q11:** Mages can co-perform the Rebinding alongside Aldren and the cleric (if any). This grants a unique permanent magical specialization.
**Q12:** Magical players have additional mechanics during the binding-tone phases of the boss fight.

## V.5 — THE BELLBINDERS AS A LOST CLASS

The Seven Bellbinders were not a class players can take — they are extinct. But the *legacy* of Bellbinding is something the game's main quest gestures toward.

A player who completes the main quest along the Rebind path receives a long-term title and access to a unique post-game progression track called **The Listening Heir**. This is a series of post-Bellbound content adventures that gradually teach the player the basics of Bellbinding magic. By the time expansion content arrives, players who completed the Listening Heir track are positioned to participate in the binding rituals at the other six Bellward sites.

This is intentional. The main quest is the *origin story* of the player as a future Bellbinder. The expansion is *the work*.

## V.6 — DEATH, RESURRECTION, AND TRUE DEATH

In Harthmere's lore, there are three states:

| State | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Alive** | Standard. | Default. |
| **Sleeping (chapel resurrection)** | The soul is briefly with Saint Verena. The body is reanimated by Verenine prayer. Costly. | Standard in MMO. |
| **True dead** | The soul has passed beyond. Cannot be returned. | Rare; reserved for narrative deaths (Bree Thistle, Halene Brae, Tovin's son, certain main quest NPCs). |

The town treats resurrection as a sacred procedure, not a casual one. The chapel's "resurrection well" is the site where players who die nearby return. Repeated deaths trigger NPC commentary — Aldren will gently chide a player whose body has appeared at the well three times in a single day.

### Ghosts and Lingering Dead

A small percentage of the dead linger — not as undead, but as *attentive* presences. The Verenine clergy do not consider them dangerous. Mother Halene Brae is one such lingering presence. Players who complete certain side quests (SQ-040) can hear her speak.

## V.7 — THE FIVE FACTIONS AS BELIEF SYSTEMS

It is worth restating the five town factions through the lens of what each one *believes*. This shapes how their members speak, what they value, and how they react to the Bellbound revelation.

### Town Watch — Belief: Order Is Mercy

The Watch believes that disorder kills more people than tyranny. A patrolled gate, a working ledger, a kept promise — these are the things that keep a town alive. The Watch is suspicious of magic, faith, and politics but respectful of all three. They will not stand in the way of the chapel. They will not arrest Caldus without orders. They will, when asked, die at the gates.

In the main quest: the Watch wants the dragon *dealt with* by any means necessary. They lean Slay.

### Merchant Compact — Belief: The Bargain Is Sacred

The Compact's faith is in the spoken word and the signed page. A bargain made honestly is a god to them. They consider broken contracts the worst of sins. This makes them frequently complicit in cruelty — debt collected from the starving is, to them, still a sacred obligation — but it also makes them unexpectedly principled at moments.

In the main quest: the Compact wants the dragon *managed* — an asset, perhaps, or a contained risk. They tend toward Rebind.

### Chapel Circle — Belief: Listening Is Love

The Chapel's faith is described above. In the main quest: the Chapel is divided. Aldren leans Rebind. Maelle leans toward whatever is most merciful to all parties — which depends on what the player learns.

### River Knots — Belief: The River Sees

The River Knots are smugglers and river-folk. They believe the river itself is conscious and that everything the river carries — bodies, secrets, goods, songs — is somehow remembered. They are nominally criminals but often deeply ethical in their own way. They do not steal from the desperate. They do not betray a passenger.

In the main quest: the Knots want the dragon *freed*, if it is to harm no one. Many of them know fragments of the Old Pact through river-songs. They lean Wake — with the Free Passage outcome.

### Mudden Kin — Belief: We Are Already Saved

The Mudden Kin do not believe in any single god, but they believe in each other. Their faith is the certainty that the poor will look after the poor when no one else will. Their values are loyalty, sharing, and survival.

In the main quest: the Mudden Kin are skeptical of all the grand plans. They will support whichever path causes least harm to their people. They are the swing faction; their support can be earned by any of the three paths.

## V.8 — THE MOON, THE STARS, AND TIME

A brief note for environmental and cosmological consistency:

The world has **one moon**, called *Vel*, the same name in nearly every regional language. Vel is silver-white, slightly more than half Earth's moon-size, and goes through standard phases over a 29-day cycle.

The stars are largely those of a fantasy night sky. Three constellations matter narratively:

- **The Listening Maid** — Verena's constellation. Visible in spring and autumn.
- **The Long Wyrm** — a long serpentine star-pattern that the Bellbinders associated with bound dragons. When it crosses the zenith on a moonless night, Bellbinder rites are most potent.
- **The Hearth-Stone** — a cluster representing home. The Compact venerates it.

The year is **372 days** in twelve months. Calendar names exist but are not detailed here.

## V.9 — A WORD ON GENDER, FAMILY, AND DAILY LIFE

The setting is not modeled on any single historical culture and does not impose modern or historical constraints uniformly. In Harthmere:

- Women serve in the Town Watch (Maelle was once a recruit; the gate guards include several women).
- Inheritance is by named heir, not by gender.
- The chapel ordains regardless of sex or background.
- Marriage is a civil contract (with optional chapel blessing). Same-sex partnerships are common and unremarkable.
- Halflings, dwarves, and half-elves are integrated rather than ghettoized.
- Children's upbringing is communal in Mudden Ward and family-private on Noble Rise.

These are baseline assumptions for writers and quest designers. The town is *medieval-flavored*, not historically medieval.

# PART VI — THE WIDER CONTINENT

This section sets up the world beyond Harthmere — the continent of **Vaerlund**, the seven Bellwards, the kingdoms that contain them, and the post-Thaedryn expansion hook. This material should not be wholly exposed to players during the Harthmere main quest. Most of it is reserved for the moment of the post-quest cinematic and for expansion content. But writers and designers need to know it now, so that every reference seeded during Harthmere's main quest is consistent with what is coming.

---

## VI.1 — THE CONTINENT OF VAERLUND

Vaerlund is a roughly heart-shaped landmass, several thousand miles north-to-south, with a mountainous spine called the **Karagrath** running through its center. The continent is bordered to the east by the **Greatsea**, to the west by the **Cold Ocean**, and to the south by a desert called the **Aethal**.

The continent has roughly **nineteen recognized polities** — kingdoms, free cities, league-states, and tribal confederations. Seven of these polities contain a Bellward. Each polity governs itself, but most acknowledge the loose authority of a continental institution called **The Listening Council** — a body of Verenine clergy and former Bellbinder kin that meets every seven years.

The Listening Council is, technically, the only institution that knows the Bellbinders' work in full. In practice, the Council has been steadily losing knowledge for centuries.

---

## VI.2 — THE SEVEN BELLWARDS

The seven dragons were bound roughly **nine hundred years ago**, during what is now called **the Long Silencing**. Each binding was performed by one Bellbinder over the course of nineteen days, supported by approximately forty Verenine clergy and an unnamed but apparently significant number of druids and ordinary townsfolk. The bindings were performed in sequence, beginning at Aevithsford and ending at Khaem.

The bindings have held — with one exception, occasional troubles, and one impending crisis — for nine centuries.

Each binding has a different *character*, because each dragon is different, and each Bellbinder approached the binding with their own art. Below is a full table of the seven Bellwards. This information is critical for expansion design.

### Table: The Seven Bellwards

| # | Town/City | Kingdom | Dragon | Dragon's Nature | Binding Style | Current Threat Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aevithsford** | Kingdom of Vellantyne | **Aevith the Sun-Eater** | Fire wyrm; furious, ancient, vengeful | "Loud" — daily bell-ringing keeps her drowsy | Stable but loud |
| 2 | **Drathmar** | Free City of Drathmar | **Karag-Drath the Mountain's Tongue** | Earth wyrm; slow, vast, mineral-minded | "Buried" — bell sealed in deep stone, rarely rung | Stable |
| 3 | **Harthmere** | Marches of Eldermere | **Thaedryn the Bellbound** | River-stone wyrm; kind, lonely | "Soft" — bell rang once a generation, kept by chapel | **CRITICAL** (player intervenes) |
| 4 | **Aetherspire** | Skylock Confederation | **Vyrenia of the High Air** | Storm wyrm; capricious, intelligent, cruel | "High" — bell hung at altitude, rung by wind | Stable, increasingly capricious |
| 5 | **Pellmarra** | Pellmaran League | **Murvath the Drinking Shadow** | Shadow wyrm; hungry, patient, hidden | "Layered" — multiple bells in concentric rings | **Weakening** (post-Harthmere story) |
| 6 | **Eshen** | Free City of Eshen | **Sylenne the Tideless** | Sea wyrm; old, sad, half-asleep | "Tidal" — bell rung by tide-machines | Stable but mourning |
| 7 | **Khaem** | Khaemite Republic | **Korruthax the Last Roar** | Storm/fire hybrid wyrm; the youngest, the angriest | "Bound twice" — extra layer of binding added 600 years ago | **Volatile** |

### VI.2.1 — Aevithsford and Aevith

**Setting:** A sun-baked walled city in the southern plains of Vellantyne, surrounded by wheat fields and olive groves. Architecture is white stone, terra-cotta tile, and gold leaf. The city is famous for its **Bellsworn Choir** — a permanent chorus of one hundred singers who maintain the daily ringing.

**The Dragon:** Aevith is fury given scale. She is the largest of the seven, perhaps a hundred and twenty feet from snout to tail-tip, her hide the color of cooled lava with veins of molten gold. She was bound while raging — her binding required the chorus to ring continuously for *seventy days*, and the tradition holds that the chorus has never since stopped. (In practice the chorus rings every dawn for two hours; the "continuous" claim is poetic.)

She remembers everything. She is conscious beneath the stones. She hates the binding and the binders both. If she is ever loosed, she will burn Vellantyne to ash and then fly north to consume the other Bellwards in retribution.

**Current state:** Stable. The Bellsworn Choir is well-funded, well-trained, and venerated. The kingdom of Vellantyne is, in fact, organized largely around the chorus's needs.

**Expansion role:** Aevith is the *biggest* threat. The expansion may save her for a final-tier raid encounter, with the player potentially needing to talk her down rather than fight her — an outcome only possible if Thaedryn was Woken with Free Passage.

---

### VI.2.2 — Drathmar and Karag-Drath

**Setting:** A vast trade city in a high mountain pass, built into and onto a mountain ridge. The architecture is grey stone, granite, and slate. Drathmar is wealthy on the back of mineral wealth (silver, copper, iron, and rare earths) extracted from mountains it does not realize are alive.

**The Dragon:** Karag-Drath is slow. He thinks in geology. A single thought of his takes a year. He is bound *beneath* Drathmar in the deepest reachable stone, his binding bell hung in a sealed chamber that has not been opened in three hundred years. The dwarves of the Karag-Drath quarter are the official wardens; they sing into the stone monthly in a rite called *the Stone's Lullaby*.

Karag-Drath is, in some sense, content. He is dreaming. He has integrated the binding into himself. The risk with him is not waking — it is *over-mining*. If Drathmar mines too deeply, they may strike his actual body. This has been forbidden by Drathmar law for six centuries.

**Current state:** Stable. The risk is purely economic — wealthy mining families occasionally agitate for permission to mine deeper.

**Expansion role:** Drathmar is a "puzzle" Bellward. The player must navigate political intrigue to prevent Drathmar's miners from accidentally waking Karag-Drath. The threat is slow but enormous.

---

### VI.2.3 — Harthmere and Thaedryn

(See Parts I–III above. This is the player's home.)

---

### VI.2.4 — Aetherspire and Vyrenia

**Setting:** A vertically-built city of towers and bridges atop a mile-high plateau, in the Skylock Confederation. Architecture is pale stone, copper, and a strange windsong material — sheets of metal alloyed to ring in the wind. The city is bordered by perpetual cloud.

**The Dragon:** Vyrenia is the wyrm of storms. She is intelligent — perhaps the cleverest of the seven — and she takes a personal interest in the affairs of Aetherspire. She is bound *above* the city: her bell is hung at the peak of the tallest tower, and the wind itself rings it. The binding is therefore *passive* — it requires no human action, only that the wind continues to blow.

The problem: Vyrenia *talks* to certain Aetherspire residents in dreams. She has cultivated a small network of confidants over centuries. She is, in essence, running a political agenda from inside her own binding.

**Current state:** Stable, but increasingly worrying. The Confederation's Wise Council has been quietly investigating "Vyrenia's whispers" for a generation.

**Expansion role:** Aetherspire is the *political-intrigue* Bellward. The player must uncover Vyrenia's dream-network and decide how to neutralize her without breaking the binding.

---

### VI.2.5 — Pellmarra and Murvath

**Setting:** A coastal trade city in the Pellmaran League, on a humid southern coast. Architecture is white stucco, dark wood, and brass. Pellmarra is famous for its layered bells — six bells in nested rings, all of which must be rung in correct sequence weekly.

**The Dragon:** Murvath the Drinking Shadow. He drinks light. Where he passes, color fades. His binding is unusual: six concentric bells, each tuned to a slightly different frequency, all sustaining a complex harmonic. To weaken his binding, only one bell needs to fail.

**Current state:** **Weakening.** The Pellmaran League has been hiding the deterioration. The shadow at the bottom of Pellmarra's central well has been spreading. The "shadow on the well" mentioned in SQ-003 is from a village near Pellmarra — the first sign of overspill.

**Expansion role:** Pellmarra is the *direct sequel* to Harthmere's main quest. Whatever happened at Harthmere (the bell partially restored, the dragon slain, the dragon woken with passage) has consequences for Pellmarra. The lattice of binding works as a system; one Bellward's outcome affects the others.

If the player Rebound Thaedryn at Harthmere, Pellmarra's deterioration *slows*. If they Slew Thaedryn, the lattice is stressed and Pellmarra accelerates. If they Woke Thaedryn with Free Passage, Pellmarra is in a strange state — the lattice has a gap, and Murvath is *aware* of it.

---

### VI.2.6 — Eshen and Sylenne

**Setting:** A free port city on Vaerlund's east coast, built on a series of islands connected by stone causeways. Architecture is sea-weathered stone, painted wood, and copper. Eshen is famous for its **Tidewheel** — a vast machine that uses the tides to ring a submerged bell on a precise schedule.

**The Dragon:** Sylenne is *old*. Older even than the binding. She was bound while already half-asleep, and she has been deeply asleep for centuries. The Verenine theory is that Sylenne *consented* to her binding — she is the only dragon for whom this is suspected.

She is sad. She mourns. She dreams of the sea before there were people. She is essentially harmless if undisturbed.

**Current state:** Stable. The Tidewheel is maintained by a hereditary guild. The risk is mechanical failure.

**Expansion role:** Eshen is the *contemplative* Bellward. The player can speak with Sylenne (with Veneth's help) and learn the deep history of the Bellbinders. Eshen's quest line is less action-heavy and more revelatory.

---

### VI.2.7 — Khaem and Korruthax

**Setting:** A young republican city in Vaerlund's far north, built on the ruins of an older city. Architecture is mixed — old fortress walls, new civic buildings, debate halls. Khaem is famous for its scholarly traditions.

**The Dragon:** Korruthax is the youngest, the angriest, and the only dragon whose binding required a *second* working. The original binding, performed by a Bellbinder named **Aldrin Stone-Ear** (no relation to Father Aldren), was insufficient. Three hundred years later, Aldrin's great-granddaughter performed a supplementary binding. Korruthax is *doubly bound* — and consequently, twice as dangerous if either binding fails.

Korruthax's nature is hybrid: half storm, half fire. He is articulate and cruel. He has, occasionally, spoken aloud through cracks in the binding. His pronouncements are recorded in a sealed Khaemite archive called the *Wyrm Codex*.

**Current state:** Volatile. The double binding is technically intact but Khaemite scholars have noted increasing irregularities. The Republic does not want to admit this publicly.

**Expansion role:** Khaem is the **climactic** Bellward of the expansion — the final test before facing Aevith (or alongside her). Korruthax is the most "dragon-like" of the seven for combat purposes: classic raid encounter scaling, multiple phases, vast spell repertoire.

---

## VI.3 — THE LISTENING COUNCIL

The continental body that nominally oversees the Bellwards. Currently composed of:

- **High Listener Vellanin Mell** (no relation to Father Aldren, despite shared surname — common Verenine name) — head of the council, age 73, based in Aetherspire.
- **Six Wardens** — one from each Bellward town except Harthmere, which has gone unrepresented for two generations since Mother Halene Brae's death.
- **Three Druidic Observers** — sent by the Old Wood, of whom Veneth is one (though she rarely attends).
- **A Recording Clerk** — currently a half-elven scholar named **Iren Vellmoor**, age 89.

The Council has been ineffective for the past century. Its meetings have become formal rather than functional. The crisis at Harthmere is the first emergency that will rouse the Council to actual coordinated action.

The Council's failure to send a Warden to Harthmere after Halene Brae's death is — the expansion will reveal — not bureaucratic neglect. It is the result of a faction *within* the Council who wishes the Harthmere binding to fail. This faction is led by a figure called **The Quiet One** — identity unknown to the player at the start of the expansion.

---

## VI.4 — THE OTHER KINGDOMS AND POWERS

Brief sketches of the major polities that contain or border the Bellwards:

### The Marches of Eldermere

Harthmere's own kingdom. A border march — historically established to guard against threats from the Old Wood. Capital city is **Eldermere**, a four-day ride north. The king is **King Aerel III**, age 54, a competent but distant ruler. The Marches do not know about the Bellbinders — only the Eldermere court chapel knows.

The Marches will, after the Harthmere main quest, send investigators. Caldus Merrow will host them — disastrously or skillfully, depending on his post-quest state.

### The Skylock Confederation (Aetherspire)

A federation of high-altitude city-states. Politically the most stable on the continent. Their currency, the *Skylock Crown*, is the de facto trade standard.

### The Pellmaran League (Pellmarra)

A coastal trade league. Wealthy, decadent, in slow decline. The League's deterioration mirrors Murvath's.

### The Khaemite Republic (Khaem)

A young experiment in republican governance. Idealistic, fractious, scholarly. Khaem produces the continent's best historians, philosophers, and engineers.

### The Free City of Drathmar (Drathmar)

A merchant republic on the mountain pass. Rich, conservative, dwarf-influenced.

### The Free City of Eshen (Eshen)

A port city with no clear governmental philosophy — run by guilds, councils, and consensus. Eshen is the most "loose" of the seven Bellward polities and probably the happiest.

### The Kingdom of Vellantyne (Aevithsford)

A sun-soaked southern kingdom. Hierarchical. The Bellsworn Choir is essentially a state religion.

### Other Polities

The continent has twelve other polities, most distant from the Bellwards. Among them:

- **The Northborn Confederations** — nomadic clans in the far north, ancestral home of Northborn humans.
- **The Halfling Reaches** — a loose collection of farmland communities south of Harthmere.
- **The Dwarven Holds** — three remaining Dwarven mountain-kingdoms.
- **The Wood Elf Glades** — barely-acknowledged elf communities scattered through the Old Wood.
- **The Aethal Tribes** — desert peoples of the south.
- **The Sea Princes** — semi-mythical island traders of the Greatsea.

## VI.5 — THE POST-THAEDRYN HOOK

What does the world look like immediately after the Harthmere main quest?

### Day One

- The Harthmere bell has been restored / Thaedryn slain / Thaedryn freed (depending on player choice).
- News begins to spread. Couriers leave Harthmere within hours.
- Father Aldren writes to the Listening Council.
- Caldus Merrow writes to King Aerel.
- The Mudden Kin throw a wake or a celebration, depending on the outcome.

### Day Seven

- A courier from Pellmarra arrives. The League knows. They are alarmed (Slay/Wake) or relieved (Rebind).
- A Skylock envoy is dispatched.
- Veneth the Moss-Woman travels east. The player may or may not know why.

### Day Thirty

- The Listening Council convenes an emergency session — the first in forty years.
- Khaemite scholars publish a treatise on "The Harthmere Event" that will become the standard text.
- A senior Verenine cleric named **Loren Vaskara** arrives in Harthmere to assess. She is, secretly, an agent of The Quiet One faction.

### Day Ninety

- The Pellmarra bells begin to *visibly* fail. The shadow at the well-bottom spreads beyond the old containment.
- A Pellmaran embassy arrives in Harthmere asking for the player by name.
- **The expansion begins.**

### Expansion Title (Tentative)

*Harthmere: The Long Silencing* — picking up from the Bellbinders' original work. The player travels from Harthmere to each of the remaining six Bellwards. The expansion's structure is hub-based: Harthmere remains the player's home, and the player returns between each Bellward visit. The order in which the player visits the other Bellwards is up to them, with the exception of Khaem, which is gated behind the others.

The expansion's antagonist is **The Quiet One** — revealed in the final acts to be **Loren Vaskara**, the cleric who came to Harthmere on Day Thirty. Her motive: she believes the bindings are a continuing moral atrocity, and she intends to free all seven dragons regardless of consequence. Whether she is right or wrong is, very deliberately, left ambiguous.

## VI.6 — THE PLAYER'S WORLD-CHANGING ROLE

It is worth being explicit about what the player *is*, narratively, by the end of the Harthmere main quest.

The player is one of perhaps three living humans who can hear the bells in the way a Bellbinder could. The player has touched Thaedryn — slain him, bound him, or freed him. The player carries either a slain dragon's tooth, a Bellbinder's pin, or a wandering wyrm's gratitude.

The player is, in short, the most important magical figure on the continent of Vaerlund — and almost nobody knows it yet.

The expansion exists to make this fact public.

---

## VI.7 — A NOTE ON TONE FOR EXPANSION CONTENT

The Harthmere main quest is *intimate* — a town, a chapel, a tunnel, a dragon. The expansion should preserve this intimacy even as the geography expands. Each Bellward should feel like *its own Harthmere* — a community, a faction structure, a set of NPCs the player comes to love.

The expansion should resist the temptation to become Big and Cosmic. The Bellbinders' work was small and slow. The player's work should remain so. The dragons are vast, but the binding is performed in chapel by candlelight.

# PART VII — PRODUCTION NOTES

This final section covers the practical concerns of shipping the Harthmere main quest as MMO content: balance bands, phasing, group sizing, cinematic moments, accessibility, content warnings, telemetry hooks, and content-update strategy.

---

## VII.1 — LEVEL AND BALANCE BANDS

The Bellbound main quest is structured for a player who enters Harthmere as a new character (level 1) and completes the quest at approximately level 12–15. Each quest scales within a band:

| Quest | Hour | Target Level | Difficulty Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 — Cracks in the Bridge | H1 | 1–3 | Tutorial. Trivial combat. |
| Q2 — Whispers at the Well | H1 | 2–4 | Light combat, exploration. |
| Q2.5 — The Rat-Girl Knows | H1 (opt) | 3–4 | Sneak-favored. |
| Q3 — Dreams of Drowning | H2 | 3–5 | Atmosphere. Minor combat. |
| Q4 — The Sister's Letters | H2 | 4–6 | Investigation. No combat. |
| Q5 — Beneath the Stones | H3 | 5–7 | Solo dungeon. Light combat. |
| Q6 — The Hidden Door | H3 | 6–8 | Public event tier. Medium combat. |
| Q7 — The Bellward Halls | H4 | 7–9 | Solo dungeon, several minibosses. |
| Q8 — Voices in the Stone | H4 | 8–10 | Choice unlock. No combat. |
| Q9 — The Veins of the Wyrm | H5 | 9–11 | **5-player group dungeon.** |
| Q10 — The Bellbinder's Tomb | H5 | 10–12 | **5-player group dungeon.** |
| Q11 — The Last Ringing | H6 | 11–13 | Solo, atmospheric. |
| Q12 — Thaedryn the Bellbound | H6 | 12–15 | **5- to 10-player raid encounter.** |

### Designer Guidelines for Tuning

- **Solo content** should be completable by any class without companion NPCs (except where NPCs are scripted in).
- **Group content** (Q9, Q10, Q12) should be tuned for 5-player parties at the low end and a flexible 10-player raid at the high end of the band.
- **Hour estimates** assume a player who reads dialogue and explores reasonably. A speed-runner will complete the quest in 3.5–4 hours. A leisurely player will spend 8+ hours.

---

## VII.2 — PHASING STRATEGY

A core MMO design challenge: how does a player who has just slain Thaedryn pass another player who is just starting Q1?

### Phasing Rules

- **Harthmere town surface** is **never fully phased**. All players see the same town. NPCs may have *additional* dialogue lines for players in different quest states, but the world geometry is shared.
- **Underways and Bellward Halls** are **instanced** when the player enters during main quest progression. After main quest completion, they remain accessible as repeatable content with rotating affixes.
- **The Bellbinder's Tomb** is **partially phased**. Surface entrance is shared. Interior chambers are instanced per group.
- **Thaedryn's Chamber (Q12)** is **fully instanced**. The dragon is "in" multiple instances simultaneously, narratively explained as "the dragon's awareness is everywhere the bell-tone reaches."
- **Post-quest Thaedryn**: the dragon's chamber remains accessible as a memorial/raid instance. On the Slay path, his bones are still there. On the Rebind path, his sleeping form can be visited. On the Wake path, his chamber is empty but his voice still occasionally echoes from the walls.

### Phase Markers on NPCs

NPC dialogue lines should branch by major quest state:

- Pre-Q1
- Mid-main-quest (specific Hour markers)
- Post-Q12 Rebind path
- Post-Q12 Slay path
- Post-Q12 Wake path
- Long after main quest completion

This is twice the work of unphased NPCs, but it is what makes the town feel responsive. Budget accordingly.

## VII.3 — CINEMATIC MOMENTS

The main quest has several scripted cinematic moments. These should be high-quality but technically scalable.

### Critical Cinematics

| Quest | Scene | Type | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | First view of the cracked bridge stone | In-engine camera pan | 8 sec |
| Q3 | The dream sequence with Thaedryn's voice | Stylized illustrated panels with VO | 45 sec |
| Q4 | Halene Brae's journal entries | Text + ambient audio | Variable |
| Q5 | First descent into the Bellward Halls | In-engine flythrough | 20 sec |
| Q6 | The hidden door opening | In-engine | 12 sec |
| Q7 | The first Bellbinder's relic | In-engine + VO | 30 sec |
| Q8 | The Three Paths revelation | Town hall cinematic | 90 sec |
| Q9 | Veins of the Wyrm — first sight of scale | In-engine | 15 sec |
| Q10 | The Tomb opens | In-engine + ritual | 45 sec |
| Q11 | The bell's final tuning / sharpening / un-ringing | Class-specific in-engine | 60–90 sec |
| Q12 | First view of Thaedryn | In-engine boss reveal | 45 sec |
| Q12 (end) | Path-specific resolution cinematic | Major cinematic | 2–3 min |

The end-of-quest cinematic should be the most polished asset in the entire main quest. It is the player's *reward* and the *hook* for the expansion.

### Three Variants of the Final Cinematic

**Rebind path:** Thaedryn falls asleep. The bell tone fades. The chamber goes still. The player is shown holding the Bellbinder's pin. Cut to: a courier on the road, riding north, with news. Cut to: a candle being lit in Pellmarra. Voice-over from the Listening Council. Final shot: Aldren weeping with relief in the chapel.

**Slay path:** Thaedryn dies. The dragon's body settles. The chamber is silent in a different way. The player holds a dragon's tooth. Cut to: the town above, oblivious. Cut to: Pellmarra, where Murvath stirs. Voice-over: "We are stronger now. We are also weaker. The lattice was not designed for one less voice." Final shot: the player walking out of the chamber alone.

**Wake path:** Thaedryn rises. The bell is broken. The dragon speaks: "Thank you." He leaves through a vast crack in the chamber wall, moving north, vanishing into the river. Cut to: Veneth, watching from the forest, smiling. Cut to: Pellmarra, where Murvath senses the absence. Voice-over from Veneth: "The first child has come home. There will be others." Final shot: the player standing in the Veyne river, looking up at sky as a distant shadow passes.

## VII.4 — VOICE ACTING

For an MMO of this scope, full voice acting for every NPC is not financially or logistically realistic. The recommended approach:

| Tier | NPCs | Voice Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Fully Voiced** | Aldren, Bram, Mara, Caldus, Elowen, Osric, Nessa, Ysabet, Tovin, Edrik, Walt, Veneth, Thaedryn | All dialogue voiced. |
| **Tier 2 — Key Lines** | Maelle, Selka, Garrik, Helna, Halpen, Tessen, Rusk, the children (Tam, Lina, Sora, Mira) | Key lines voiced; others text. |
| **Tier 3 — Greetings/Reactions** | All other named NPCs | One or two voiced phrases on approach; text dialogue. |
| **Tier 4 — Ambient** | Crowd, unnamed townsfolk | Generic vocal sets; text. |

Thaedryn's voice is a special technical challenge. The voice should be:
- Deep but not growled
- Layered (two voices speaking in unison, slightly offset)
- Resonant (as if speaking from inside stone)
- Capable of sounding *tired* and *kind* simultaneously

This is achievable through doubling and reverb on a single skilled performer. Suggested reference: a low-register actor who can convey gentleness — not a "monster voice" type.

## VII.5 — ACCESSIBILITY

The main quest must be accessible to a wide range of players.

### Reading and Hearing

- All voiced dialogue has subtitles.
- All ambient sound cues (bell tones, whispers) have visual cues for hearing-impaired players (a faint blue ripple at the screen edge when a bell tone plays; iconography for whispered voices).
- Text size scalable.

### Motor

- All click-and-hold mechanics have a hold-toggle alternative.
- Quick-time events in cinematics are optional or skippable.
- Combat in Q12 has a slower "story difficulty" tier for solo players who cannot reliably play with a group.

### Visual

- Color-blind-safe palette for key UI elements (faction colors, quest markers).
- Important objects in dim environments have a subtle outline option.

### Cognitive

- Quest journal entries are written in plain prose with bullet objectives.
- A "Where do I go next?" hint button is available on any quest.
- Lore content is *opt-in* — players who skip dialogue should still be able to complete the quest mechanically. Players who engage with dialogue gain deeper context and bonus rewards.

## VII.6 — CONTENT WARNINGS

The Bellbound main quest contains the following content. These should be flagged for players who wish to know in advance.

- **Death:** Discussions of grief, including the death of children (Tam's siblings, Tovin's son, the rumor of Halene Brae). Treated soberly, not graphically.
- **Religious content:** Verenine theology, depictions of clergy, depictions of confession and ritual.
- **Imprisonment / binding:** Thaedryn's binding can be read as a metaphor for various real-world situations. Players who choose Rebind are participating in what some might consider an ethical compromise. This is intentional and is part of the moral weight of the quest.
- **Mental health:** Aldren's grief and self-harm-adjacent thoughts (he refers to wanting to "drown in the river" once, in confessional dialogue). Treated as the heavy weight of duty, not glamorized.
- **Class and poverty:** Mudden Ward content depicts poverty, eviction, child neglect (though never abuse onscreen), and class injustice. Depicted with sympathy.
- **Animal harm:** Hunting and the killing of corrupted animals occurs. Standard MMO fantasy.
- **Violence:** Combat throughout. Boss encounter is dramatic but not gory.

The quest does NOT contain: sexual content, depictions of sexual violence, depictions of self-harm enacted onscreen, racism or in-world ethnic conflict, or graphic body horror.

## VII.7 — TELEMETRY HOOKS

For long-term content health, the following data points should be tracked:

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Q1 → Q12 completion rate | Quest funnel health |
| Per-quest time-to-completion | Difficulty tuning |
| Per-quest abandonment rate | Identifies frustration points |
| Q8 path selection distribution | Are players choosing meaningfully? Target: ~40/40/20 split (any path under 15% needs revisiting) |
| Q12 wipe rate by group composition | Boss tuning |
| Dialogue read rate (where measurable) | Identifies skipped or "burnt out" sections |
| NPC interaction frequency post-quest | Are players forming lasting relationships? |
| Side quest pickup rate | Which NPCs are players bonding with? Inform expansion focus. |
| Faction reputation distribution | Are factions feeling distinct? |
| Bell-Tie side quest completion before main quest | Players using the system as intended? |

## VII.8 — CONTENT UPDATE STRATEGY

Once the Bellbound main quest is live, treat it as **load-bearing infrastructure**, not as content to retire.

### Sustaining Updates

- **Seasonal:** River Blessing, Harvest Fair, Night of Ashes, Bridge Day — each updates Harthmere visually and adds 1–2 new quests in the town's existing systems.
- **Monthly:** New side quests, new faction repeatables, new dialogue lines for existing NPCs reacting to seasonal events.
- **Quarterly:** A new "Underways affix" for Q9 and Q10 repeatable content (e.g., "Flooded," "Bell-Sick," "Smuggler-Held").
- **Annual:** Anniversary of the Bellbound event — a special public event commemorating the player's chosen path.

### Major Expansions

- **Year 1:** The expansion described in Part VI. Travel to the other six Bellwards.
- **Year 2:** "The Long Silencing II" — return to Harthmere with new threats; introduces the Bellbinder class as playable.
- **Year 3:** A continental crisis tied to Aevith.

### What NOT to do

- Do not retcon the player's main quest choice. The three paths must remain canonical for that player.
- Do not "fix" the moral ambiguity of the binding. The discomfort is the point.
- Do not turn Thaedryn into a cartoon villain in retrospective content. He is, and remains, a tragic figure.
- Do not allow expansion content to inflate the player's identity to mythic levels. The player is special, but Harthmere is the world they came from, and that world should remain small and human.

## VII.10 — CLOSING NOTE TO THE DESIGN TEAM

Harthmere is, at heart, the story of a town and a dragon. Both deserve to be loved.

The town should feel like somewhere worth saving — not because it is grand, but because it is small and ordinary and full of people whose names the player will remember. Bram with his sick daughter. Mara who lost her own daughter and still opens her market stall every day. Aldren who has carried a secret for three years and is finally allowed to put it down. Tovin who keeps two ledgers and a grief for his drowned son. Nessa who counts the children. Old Tam who plays the same tune every evening at the inn.

The dragon, likewise, should feel like someone the player has met. Not a trophy. Not a final boss in the dismissive sense. A being who was bound when the world was younger, who has been listening to the town above him for nine hundred years, who knows their songs and their bell-tones and the names of their children. A creature who will, in three of the game's three possible endings, do the right thing — by his own understanding.

If we build Harthmere well, the player will remember it after they have moved on to expansion content. They will return for the festivals. They will check on Bram's daughter. They will visit Aldren at the chapel. They will, occasionally, descend back into the Underways — empty now, or sleeping, or echoing — and stand for a moment, listening.

That is the goal. Build a town worth listening to.

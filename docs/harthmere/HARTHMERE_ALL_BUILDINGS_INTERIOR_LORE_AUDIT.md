# Harthmere All-Buildings Interior and Furniture Lore Audit

## Purpose and scope

This is a design audit only. It does not authorize code changes, Blender generation, world rebuilding, or placement edits.

The audit covers:

- all 57 canonical structures in `HARTHMERE_TOWN_BUILDINGS`;
- the 19 procedural Harthmere business buildings, whose approved bespoke interiors already establish the quality bar;
- the two Chapter 1 Grove buildings that form the Harthmere connector's immediate story architecture.

Player-created plots are variable templates rather than fixed authored buildings, so they receive principles rather than individual room briefs. Deep Underways halls are dungeon spaces, not surface buildings, and should receive a separate dungeon-interior audit.

### Evidence labels

- **Lore-locked:** explicitly supported by the MMO Town Design Bible, Bellbound story bible, named NPC residence/workplace data, quest staging, or an existing building brief.
- **Role-derived:** a design recommendation inferred from the building's name, district, footprint, material palette, and town economy. It should remain compatible with lore, but it is not a quoted canon detail.
- **Approved business brief:** already defined in the accepted 19-business asset pipeline.

## Executive findings

1. **The 57-building furniture pass is safe but too generic.** The current system chooses from nine voxel patterns - bed, table/bench, shelf, crates, counter, hearth, anvil, straw bunk, and barrels - by matching the building name/profile. This protects doors and stairs, but does not express the actual lore of most buildings.
2. **The 19 business interiors are the correct quality benchmark.** They have profession-specific room zoning, customer/staff separation, visible stock, signature machinery, upper-floor uses, and audited routes. The remaining public buildings and homes should reach the same level of identity without copying the business layouts.
3. **Every public building needs one entrance-readable focal cluster.** A player should recognize a gate office, chapel, mill, bank, laundry, tannery, or dock warehouse from the first room without reading a debug label.
4. **Homes need occupants, habits, and class identity.** Repeating the same bed/nightstand/chest/table/candle set in every room undermines Harthmere's strongest lore: ordinary people, class pressure, family life, work brought home, and district solidarity.
5. **Shared housing needs shared space.** The ten Residential apartment houses and four Mudden stacks should include stairs, landings, shared kitchens/hearths, wash areas, notice hooks, and household storage, not just isolated bedroom kits.
6. **Poverty must be portrayed as constrained but human.** Mudden interiors should be repaired, shared, improvised, and crowded, with evidence of mutual care. They should not be random filth or comic junk piles.
7. **Several source contracts disagree about floors or room placement.** Those conflicts must be resolved before exact furniture coordinates are authored.

## Town-wide interior language

- Use rough river stone, weathered oak, patched cloth, forged iron, warm candle/lantern light, and visibly maintained wear.
- Keep the central entrance route clear. Put large furniture against walls or in room-defining islands that do not block stairs, doors, queues, or NPC routes.
- Put small props on support surfaces. Bottles belong on shelves, ledgers on desks, tools on racks, and sacks/crates on the floor.
- Use icon silhouettes and signature objects: anvil, loaf, mortar, scale, kettle, fishhook, lantern, quill, bell, or crossed pikes.
- Wealth differences should be legible through finish and privacy, not simply quantity of clutter.
- Public buildings need waiting furniture, service furniture, staff storage, records, and at least one secured or private zone.
- Homes need sleeping, warmth, eating, storage, work/personal history, and one occupant-specific detail.
- Multi-floor buildings need a reason for each floor. Public service stays on the ground floor; private rooms, records, storage, war rooms, guest rooms, or staff quarters go upstairs.

# Part I - Canonical 57-building audit

## North Gate

### 1. North Gate West Gatehouse - 14x18, two floors

**Basis:** Lore-locked. North Gate is the arrival/security threshold: tolls, warnings, anti-smuggling checks, guard readiness, caravans, chains, pikes, and brazier light.

**Interior:** The ground floor should be the incoming-traveler inspection side: long examination table, road-warning board, confiscated-goods cage, evidence chest, bench for travelers, shield/pike rack, lantern hooks, and a small clerk ledger station. The upper floor should be a watch ready-room with compact bunks, footlockers, alarm horn/bell gear, wall map of the North Road, spare tabards, and a narrow meal table.

**Focal cue:** A red-and-black watch banner above a scarred inspection table visible from the door.

### 2. North Gate East Gatehouse - 14x18, two floors

**Basis:** Lore-locked, with role-derived asymmetry. It should not be a mirrored copy of the west tower.

## Residential apartment houses

These ten buildings should share a structural kit - entry hall, stair, shared hearth/kitchen, wash corner, household storage, and divided private rooms - but each requires a distinct occupational and emotional identity. The specific themes below are role-derived unless named residents later establish stronger canon.

### 27. Rosewall House - 20x16, two floors

Use rose-dyed textiles, a tidy shared parlor, sewing basket, small flower press, better-finished chairs, and private rooms with modest decorative keepsakes. It should feel respectable and cared for rather than wealthy.

### 28. Sunbeam House - 20x16, two floors

Use pale/yellow curtains, a bright breakfast table, children's reading bench, polished copper pan shelf, simple woven rugs, and window-oriented work surfaces. The interior should exploit daylight.

### 29. Blue Shutter House - 20x16, two floors

Use river-worker coats, blue cloth, boot racks, rope baskets, small weather journal, drying pegs, and family rooms with sea/river keepsakes. Keep it residential, not a second dock warehouse.

### 30. Chimneybend House - 20x16, two floors

Center the shared life around a large, repaired hearth: cook pots, firewood niche, chimney tools, kettle hooks, warm benches, and soot-darkened ceiling beams. Private rooms can be sparse because the communal hearth is the identity.

### 31. Lavender Lane House - 20x16, two floors

Use linen cupboards, lavender sachets, mending table, neat bedcovers, small herb-drying shelf, and a quiet shared sitting room. It should suggest domestic care work, not an apothecary business.

### 32. Brass Knocker House - 20x16, two floors

Use cleaner entry furniture, writing desks, account books, lockable cabinets, coat stand, visiting chairs, and aspirational brass details. This is suitable for clerks, junior merchants, and professionals who want to appear more prosperous than they are.

### 33. Appleblossom House - 20x16, two floors

Use orchard baskets, preserve jars, peeling/prep table, honey crock, drying fruit strings, green textiles, and a family eating table. Avoid duplicating the bakery; this is a household tied to seasonal orchard labor.

### 34. Wheatgold House - 20x16, two floors

Use grain sacks, bread boards, threshing gloves, work aprons, seed jars, lunch baskets, and a sturdy shared pantry. Flour dust and practical storage should connect residents to fields and mills.

### 35. Canalview House - 20x16, two floors

Use rain capes, nets being repaired, river charts, drying hooks, eel baskets, blue-gray textiles, and a window bench facing water. The building should feel like dock and canal families live here after work.

### 36. Miller's Rest House - 20x16, two floors

Use mill-worker boots, flour-sack cupboards, meal table, hand tools, spare belts/pegs, bread cooling shelf, and simple beds. It should bridge residential life with the mill economy without housing full machinery.

# Structural and lore conflicts to resolve before placement work

1. **Black Anvil Smithy:** the canonical 57-building table declares one floor, while the bible/build manifest and residence lore require Osric's apartment above the smithy. The final shell must either become two floors or receive a clearly separated rear/side residence.
2. **Dawn Loaf Bakery:** the building table declares one floor, but Dawn's home is described as a bakery room above. Resolve as a true loft/upper floor or change the canonical residence to a rear room.
3. **Saint Verena Chapel:** the 57-building table declares one floor, while the renderer/build manifest describes a two-floor chapel/bell tower and the story requires archive, infirmary, clergy rooms, tower access, cellar, and undercroft. The chapel needs a single authoritative vertical plan.
4. **Harthmere Stables:** the table declares one floor, while Jory's home is called a stable-yard loft. Add a usable loft or relocate his private quarters into a grounded side room.
5. **Duplicate/overlapping building systems:** some canonical footprints overlap other authored structures. Bespoke furniture must use the final materialized shell and stair/door geometry, not only nominal rectangles.
6. **Residential capacity:** ten apartment houses are described as multi-room resident quarters. Their internal partitions, shared spaces, and resident assignments must be treated as the room source of truth before individual furniture is placed.
7. **Mudden vertical stacks:** four- and five-floor stacks need proven stairs, landings, headroom, and emergency circulation before any clutter pass.

# Recommended production order for a later implementation

1. Resolve the four vertical-plan conflicts: Smithy, Bakery, Chapel, Stables.
2. Build signature interiors for story-critical public spaces: Chapel, Reeve Hall, Copper Kettle, Black Anvil, Old Well entry, Rat Crown drain house.
3. Build functional service interiors: bank, auction office, provisioner, apothecary, dock supply/warehouse, mail, tannery, mills, laundry.
4. Build shared-housing kits with real room partitions: ten Residential houses and four Mudden stacks.
5. Build named homes and Wilds shelters with occupant-specific prop variants.
6. Preserve and integrate the already-approved 19 business interiors without double-rendering their furniture.

# Acceptance standard for future art work

For every building, a later implementation should prove:

- the building's function is readable from the entrance;
- every floor has a purpose;
- furniture is grounded and supported;
- doors, stairs, landings, windows, service points, and center aisles remain clear;
- small props sit on real support surfaces;
- resident and workplace lore is visibly represented;
- district palette and wealth level are distinct;
- no generic debug blocks, mannequins, floating props, or duplicated combined/placeable furniture appear;
- public interactions remain reachable from the real in-world building.
